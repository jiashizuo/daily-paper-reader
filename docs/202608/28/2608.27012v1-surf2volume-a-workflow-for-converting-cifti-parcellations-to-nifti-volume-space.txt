# Abstract 

Parcellations distributed in Connectivity Informatics Technology Initiative (CIFTI) format cannot be used directly in 

many analysis programs that require volume input. Existing conversion options may leave voxels in cortical gray 

matter unlabeled or assign  labels outside gray matter, depending on the mapping parameters. We present 

Surf_2_Volume, a workflow that combines Connectome Workbench, FreeSurfer, AFNI, neuromaps, and Python 

image processing to convert cortical and subcortical CIFTI parcellations into  Neuroimaging Informatics Technology 

Initiative (NIfTI) volumes. The workflow separates cortical and subcortical components, transfers cortical labels 

through fsaverage and a surface representation of the target MNI152 template, restricts voxel assignment  using an 

adjustable probability threshold for gray matter, and recombines the components. Using the Cole -Anticevic Brain -

wide Network Partition, Surf_2_Volume had an adjusted Dice score of 0.776, compared with a maximum of 0.637 

among the evaluated Connect ome Workbench settings. In a separate test using the Schaefer 2018 17 -network 

volume atlas, the scores were 0.727 for Surf_2_Volume and 0.535 for the best Workbench setting. Across both atlas 

evaluations, Surf_2_Volume had higher adjusted Dice scores than  the evaluated Workbench settings. The workflow 

provides a way to use surface parcellations in software that requires NIfTI input while allowing explicit control over 

gray matter coverage. 

Keywords:  CIFTI; NIfTI; surface -to -volume mapping; brain parcellation; gray matter; MNI152 

# Introduction 

The Human Connectome Project (HCP) established large, consistently processed neuroimaging datasets that support 

studies of brain structure, function, connectivity, and topographic organization (Glasser et al. 2016b). Several widely 

used HCP -derived parcell ations were constructed within the CIFTI grayordinate framework, which represents the 

cerebral cortex as surface vertices and subcortical structures as volume voxels (Glasser et al. 2016a; Ji et al. 2019). 

This hybrid representation limits unnecessary samp ling of cortical white matter and cerebrospinal fluid and is well 

suited to surface -based analyses. 

Many established neuroimaging workflows, however, operate primarily on NIfTI volumes. Common examples 

include statistical and visualization workflows built around SPM, FSL, and MRIcroGL. A CIFTI label file cannot be 

used directly in these workflows. Public ly available projections, such as a projection of HCP -MMP1.0 to fsaverage 

(Mills 2016) and an extended HCP multimodal atlas in MNI152 space (Huang et al. 2022), address specific atlases 

or target spaces. They do not provide a general method for converting  other CIFTI parcellations that contain both 

cortical and subcortical labels. 

Connectome Workbench includes methods for mapping labels to a volume based on a cortical ribbon or the nearest 

surface vertex. The output depends on the available surfaces and mapping parameters. In the examples assessed 

here, some settings left gaps in co rtical gray matter, whereas more permissive settings assigned labels outside gray 

matter. This study therefore focused on a workflow that gives users explicit control over gray matter coverage 

during conversion. 3

Surf_2_Volume was developed for this use case. It combines existing neuroimaging tools to convert a CIFTI label 

parcellation into an NIfTI volume and lets the user set a probability threshold for gray matter. The workflow was 

evaluated with the Cole -Antice vic Brain -wide Network Partition (CAB -NP) and the Schaefer 2018 17 -network 

volume atlas. The resulting volumes support analysis and visualization in software that requires NIfTI input. 

# Materials and methods 

Source parcellation 

The primary example used the CAB -NP CIFTI parcellation. CAB -NP was derived from resting -state functional MRI 

data from 337 HCP participants and assigns cortical and subcortical locations to 12 large -scale networks (Ji et al. 

2019). The published group -leve l label file was used as the conversion input. No individual -level participant data 

were processed in the present workflow evaluation. 

Surf_2_Volume workflow 

The workflow preserves the distinction between cortical surface labels and subcortical volume labels until the final 

assembly step. It uses FreeSurfer for surface reconstruction and surface -to -surface transfer, AFNI and SUMA for 

template and surface handli ng, Connectome Workbench for CIFTI component separation, neuromaps for surface 

resampling, and Python -based image operations for volume construction. The processing sequence is as follows. 

1.  A surface representation is generated from the T1 -weighted image associated with the target MNI152 template 

using FreeSurfer. The resulting surfaces are converted to a SUMA specification that can be used in subsequent 

registration and rasterization steps. 

2.  Connectome Workbench is used to separate the input CIFTI label file into left -hemisphere and right -hemisphere 

cortical GIFTI label files and a subcortical NIfTI label file. 

3.  The cortical GIFTI labels are resampled to the fsaverage surface with neuromaps (Markello et al. 2022). Label -

preserving resampling is used so that the output remains categorical. 

4.  FreeSurfer surface registration is used to transfer the labels from fsaverage to the surface representation of the 

target MNI152 template. The mapped labels are then represented on the standard std.141 surface grid used by 

the workflow. 

5.  The cortical labels are assigned to target -space voxels within a custom gray -matter probability mask. The 

probability threshold is user adjustable. Lower thresholds include more candidate gray -matter voxels, whereas 

higher thresholds restrict the output to  voxels with greater gray -matter probability. 

6.  The left cortical, right cortical, and subcortical label volumes are combined to create a whole -brain NIfTI 

parcellation. Separate outputs are generated for MNI152NLin6Asym and MNI152NLin2009cAsym at 2 mm 

isotropic resolution.

command. The first used  -ribbon -constrained  mapping. The second used  -nearest -vertex

trade -off between cortical coverage and assignment beyond gray matter. All methods used the same input

Overlap assessment 

To quantify label preservation, each generated NIfTI atlas was projected from MNI152 volume space to the fsLR 

32k cortical surface with neuromaps (Markello et al. 2022). Nearest -neighbor interpolation was used because the 

atlas contained discrete labels. T he transformation produced separate GIFTI label maps for the left and right 

hemispheres, which were compared with the original CAB -NP GIFTI label maps. Dice coefficients are commonly 

used to quantify spatial overlap in neuroimaging (Wen et al. 2022; Zhang  et al. 2024; Zhang et al. 2025). Dice was 

calculated separately for labels 1 through 12 in each hemisphere. For label k, Dice was twice the number of vertices 

shared by the converted and reference label masks divided by the sum of the numbers of vertices i n the two masks. 

The values for the 12 labels were averaged without weighting to obtain D for each hemisphere. If a label was absent 

from both maps, its Dice coefficient was set to 1.0. Conventional Dice measures label overlap on the surface, but it 

does n ot directly account for labels placed outside gray matter or gray matter left unlabeled in the volume. We 

therefore combined D with two voxel terms to calculate an adjusted Dice score for each hemisphere.

Here, E was the proportion of labeled atlas voxels that fell outside the gray matter mask for that hemisphere. Its 

denominator was the total number of labeled atlas voxels in the hemisphere. O was the proportion of voxels within

Supplementary atlas evaluation 

For a supplementary test, the Schaefer 2018 17 -network volume atlas was used as the reference (Schaefer et al. 

2018). The reference volume was projected to the fsLR 32k cortical surface with neuromaps using nearest -neighbor 

interpolation. It was then mappe d back to volume space with Surf_2_Volume or the evaluated Connectome 

Workbench settings. Each reconstructed volume was compared with the original reference using the same procedure 

for each hemisphere. 

Dice coefficients were calculated for all 17 nonzero network labels in the reference. The unweighted mean across 

labels was calculated separately for each hemisphere before E and O were applied. Nearest -neighbor interpolation 

was used to preserve categoric al labels. 

Software compatibility check 

The generated NIfTI label files were opened in SPM, FSL, MRIcroGL, and FreeSurfer freeview. This check 

determined only whether the files could be loaded and displayed. It did not test subsequent analysis functions. 5

# Discussion 

Surf_2_Volume addresses a specific compatibility problem: an atlas distributed in CIFTI format may be difficult to 

use in software that requires NIfTI volumes. The workflow does not introduce a new registration theory or cortical 

model. It combines establi shed surface and volume tools into a defined conversion sequence and adds an adjustable 

probability threshold for gray matter. The threshold allows users to choose between wider cortical coverage and 

more restrictive voxel assignment. 

In the two evaluations, Surf_2_Volume had higher adjusted Dice scores than the tested Workbench configurations. 

This result is consistent with the adjusted score: missing labels in gray matter lower O, whereas labels outside gray 

matter increase E. 

The conversion may be useful when a surface -derived parcellation must be incorporated into an existing voxel -based 

workflow, for example to define regions in a conventional NIfTI -based analysis or to visualize a CIFTI -derived 

network atlas alongside other  volume data. Providing precomputed CAB -NP outputs in two commonly used 

template spaces reduces the amount of setup required for this example. The containerized workflow is intended to 

support conversion of other atlases, although users should verify label  integrity, target -space metadata, and coverage 

for each new input. 

# Limitations 

The evaluation has several limitations. First, it included two group -level atlas examples, two MNI152 spaces, and a 

limited set of Workbench settings. It did not include repeated conversions across individual participants. Confidence 

intervals, statistical  tests, and variation between participants therefore could not be estimated. Second, the adjusted 

Dice score combines three properties into one descriptive value and has not been validated as a general measure of 

surface -to -volume conversion quality. Repor ting D, E, and O separately in future evaluations would make the source 

of any difference clearer. 

Third, other surfaces, distances, interpolation choices, or additional processing steps could change the Workbench 

results. Fourth, the evaluation did not examine errors for individual labels, boundary distance, topological changes, 

small parcels, or atlas  resolutions beyond the reported examples. Fifth, opening the generated files in common 

software demonstrated file compatibility but did not test every subsequent analysis. The evaluation also did not 

determine a probability threshold that is optimal acros s atlases or analyses. Users should inspect each derived atlas 

and avoid interpolation that creates fractional label values. 

A final limitation is that a volume representation remains an approximation of a cortical surface parcellation. 

Cortical folding varies substantially between individuals, and volumetric registration can mix signal across nearby 8

banks of a sulcus (Coalson et al. 2018). The converted atlas should therefore be treated as a volume representation 

for compatibility. Analysis in the original surface space remains preferable when the research question depends on 

fine cortical topography,  individual anatomy, laminar organization, or submillimeter localization. 

# Conclusion 

Surf_2_Volume converts cortical and subcortical CIFTI parcellations into NIfTI volumes and allows users to control 

gray matter coverage with a probability threshold. In the CAB -NP and Schaefer 2018 17 -network examples, it had 

higher adjusted Dice scores th an the evaluated Connectome Workbench settings. The workflow provides a 

conversion option when a volume atlas and explicit control over gray matter coverage are required. It addresses a 

practical gap between surface atlas resources and software that requir es NIfTI input.
