## 摘要
创伤性脑损伤（TBI）常导致常规MRI无法检测到的细微白质损伤。扩散峰度成像（DKI）是扩散张量成像（DTI）的扩展，可提供非高斯水扩散的补充信息，并对复杂的白质微结构敏感。随着超高场MRI的出现，空间分辨率和信噪比（SNR）得到显著提升，从而能够更精确地显示细微异常。基于这些进展，我们开发了TractSpLearn，一种个体化的基于纤维束的学习框架，可同时考虑组内变异和组间差异。与仅从健康对照中学习规范性流形的原始TractLearn框架不同，TractSpLearn纳入健康对照和患者来学习共享流形，该流形具有以健康为锚定的表示以及额外的患者相关成分。为评估所提方法的性能，我们在三个队列中比较了TractSpLearn与原始TractLearn：（i）健康对照（HC），（ii）患有持续性脑震荡后综合征（PPCS）的运动员，以及（iii）有重复性头部损伤（RHI）的运动员，其中异常在轴向峰度（AK）和平均扩散率（MD）上尤为明显。在RHI中，TractSpLearn突出了患者间反复出现的异常。在PPCS队列中，整体组间差异较为有限，这可能既反映了样本量小导致的统计效力有限，也反映了恢复过程中白质改变的部分正常化。尽管如此，与TractLearn相比，TractSpLearn在更多患者中以及更多受累纤维束上识别出了异常证据。

## Abstract
Traumatic brain injury (TBI) often leads to subtle white matter damage that remains undetected on conventional MRI. Diffusion kurtosis imaging (DKI), an extension of diffusion tensor imaging (DTI), provides complementary information on non-Gaussian water diffusion and is sensitive to complex white-matter microstructure. With the advent of ultra-high-field MRI, the spatial resolution and signal-to-noise ratios (SNR) have been significantly enhanced, enabling more precise visualization of subtle abnormalities. Building on these advances, we developed TractSpLearn, an individualized tract-based learning framework that jointly considers within-group variability and between-group differences. Unlike the original TractLearn framework, which learns a normative manifold exclusively from healthy controls, TractSpLearn incorporates both healthy controls and patients to learn a shared manifold with a healthy-anchored representation and an additional patient-related component. To assess the performance of the proposed method, we compared TractSpLearn with the original TractLearn in three cohorts: (i) healthy controls (HC), (ii) athletes with persistent post-concussive syndromes (PPCS), and (iii) athletes with repeated head injuries (RHI), with abnormalities particularly evident in axial kurtosis (AK) and mean diffusivity (MD). In RHI, TractSpLearn highlighted recurrent abnormalities across patients. In the PPCS cohort, the overall group-level differences were more modest, potentially reflecting both limited statistical power due to the small sample size and partial normalization of white-matter alterations during recovery. Still TractSpLearn identified abnormality evidence in more patients and across more affected tracts than TractLearn.
