## 摘要
基于血氧水平依赖（BOLD）信号的亚毫米功能磁共振成像（fMRI）使得在亚毫米水平研究脑功能成为可能，有助于揭示皮层层次和柱状结构等精细尺度组织。然而，其固有的低对比度噪声比（CNR）和信噪比（SNR）往往限制了其可靠性和适用性。基于分布校正主成分分析的降噪方法（NORDIC PCA）是一种局部低秩去噪算法，以局部图像块方式降低 BOLD fMRI 中的热噪声水平。然而，局部图像块通常包含来自多种组织的混合信号，这会负面影响图像块的低秩结构，从而限制算法的去噪能力。我们提出了一种通过聚集相似的非局部体素来构建图像块的替代方法，称为体素匹配（VM）NORDIC。在亚毫米分辨率 BOLD fMRI 数据上的结果表明，VM-NORDIC 通过提高信号冗余度有效提升图像块的低秩性，从而实现更高效的噪声衰减。此外，由于基于时间序列相似性进行非局部体素选择，该方法几乎不影响空间平滑度。特别是，VM-NORDIC 在时间信噪比（tSNR）（比 Standard-NORDIC 大约 9-90%；比原始数据大约 23-250%）和空间平滑度估计（约为 Standard-NORDIC 引起的平滑度的 20%）方面优于使用默认局部图像块构建的标准 NORDIC（Standard-NORDIC）。这些改进对于提高亚毫米分辨率 fMRI 研究的有效性和精度至关重要。

## Abstract
Submillimeter functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) based on blood-oxygenation-level-dependent (BOLD) signal enables the study of brain function at the submillimeter level, uncovering insights into fine-scale organisations like cortical layers and columns. However, its inherently low contrast-to-noise ratio (CNR) and signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) often limit its reliability and applicability. Noise Reduction with Distribution Corrected Principal Components Analysis (NORDIC PCA) is a locally low-rank denoising algorithm that reduces thermal noise levels in BOLD fMRI in a local patch manner. However, local patches often contain a mixture of signals from multiple tissues that negatively affects the low-rank structure of the patches, which limits the denoising capabilities of the algorithm. We propose an alternative approach for patch formation by gathering similar non-local voxels, dubbed voxel-matching (VM) NORDIC. The results on submillimeter-resolution BOLD fMRI data indicate that VM-NORDIC effectively promotes the low rank of the patches by boosting signal redundancy, allowing for more efficient noise attenuation. Moreover, the method barely affects spatial smoothness due to the non-local voxel selection based on time-series similarity. In particular, VM-NORDIC outperforms standard NORDIC with default local patching (Standard-NORDIC) in terms of temporal SNR (tSNR) (~9-90% larger than Standard-NORDIC; ~23-250% larger than the original) and spatial smoothness estimates (~20% of the smoothness induced by Standard-NORDIC). These improvements are fundamental to improving the validity and precision of fMRI studies at submillimeter resolutions.
