Abstract
Functional brain connectome represents neural connectivity as a matrix of pair-
wise interactions between brain regions. Generation of functional connectomes
is not only a question of validity; rather, having satisfied the constraints on the
correlation matrix, the next step is to recover the class-conditional geometry buried
under coarse labels. We propose MAGNET, a Manifold-Aware Graph Diffusion
Network which uses a normalized-Cholesky representation of the manifold of
correlation matrices that guarantees validity. MAGNET lifts noisy latent states into
ROI-level region tokens and performs denoising with a relational inductive bias
over brain atlas regions. To deal with structural problems induced by coarse labels,
MAGNET employs class-anchored conditioning, amortized structural bridge, and
relevance-preserving corruption. Across ABIDE, ADNI, and OASIS-3, MAGNET
consistently achieves favorable results compared to previous manifold-aware ap-
proaches, demonstrating improvements of 7 −21% in class-conditional fidelity
(α, β-F1) across three cohorts and better sampling efficiency. Moreover, while
training only with strict binary labels, MAGNET is capable of maintaining clinical
heterogeneity through fine substructure of the connectomes in a zero-shot setting,
improving subclass covariance alignment (λ-MSE) by over 30%. These results
suggest that geometric validity is a necessary but insufficient condition for clinical
utility in connectome synthesis. Moreover, efforts in making the diffusion denoising
class-conditional manifold aware finds utility beyond the highly curved brain con-
nectome generation as this is a critical problem in various general settings. The code
repository can be found at https://github.com/protyayofficial/MAGNET.

Introduction
A brain connectome is a comprehensive map of neural connectivity [73], formalized as a relational
object where each entry encodes interactions between pairs of distinct brain regions. Resting-state
functional connectomes play a central role in biomarker discovery, cohort analysis, and personalized
prognosis in modern neuroimaging [71, 14]. Conditional generative model for connectomes enables
key applications in data-scarce settings, including augmentation [22], synthesis of underrepresented
populations [56], and construction of synthetic cohorts [76]. Such a model must satisfy two criterias:
(i) samples must be valid correlation matrices (symmetric, positive definite, with unit diagonals) [61,
2]; and (ii) must lie within the valid class-conditional regions of the connectome manifold, ensuring
both geometric validity and clinical consistency [7, 64, 18]. While manifold-aware generative models
address validity, accurately recovering class-conditional support remains a key challenge.
Riemannian score models approximate distributions with manifold support [23], while Riemannian
flow matching and pullback-based pipelines enable efficient transport without leaving the mani-
fold [38, 18]. Recent work on manifold-preserving diffusion, guidance, and Lie group methods

While a valid chart is nec-

All three challenges are compounded by a structural mismatch inherent to the chart: connectomes
are relational objects [6], but the normalized-Cholesky parameterization imposes a strict ordering on
brain regions, making latent coordinates explicitly not permutation-equivariant [85, 50]. Graph-based
inductive biases are therefore motivated not by permutation invariance [11] but by the structured
dependencies among ordered Cholesky coefficients. Each zij encodes the orthogonalized partial
correlation of region i with respect to the preceding j −1 regions in the atlas ordering. Moreover,
ROI-level attention with learned anatomical positional embeddings [32, 30] consistently outperforms
flat denoisers that ignore this relational structure [41, 9, 21, 10]. Together, these shift the focus
beyond geometric feasibility: the core difficulty is conditional support recovery.
Keeping the aforementioned points in mind, we introduce MAGNET, a Manifold-Aware Graph
Network diffusion framework operating in a normalized-Cholesky chart [2, 18], where denoising is
performed over region tokens rather than flattened coordinates. The central insight is that geometric
validity is a necessary but insufficient condition and the binding constraint is conditional support
recovery. It requires jointly resolving label ambiguity, missing global context, and coordinate
corruption within the valid manifold. MAGNET addresses this through three coupled mechanisms:
2


• We address the label challenge via class-anchored conditioning: each class is modeled as a
Gaussian mixture [51, 29, 24], assigning subtype tokens to explicitly factor latent structural
heterogeneity before conditioning reaches the denoiser [76, 82, 26, 86].
• For the global-context challenge, we introduce an amortized structural bridge, a lightweight
predictor that provides subtype-specific structural summaries derived from correlation statistics
at each reverse step. This compensates for the inability of local noisy states to encode global
connectivity under few-step regimes, while avoiding the diversity collapse typically induced by
classifier-free guidance in limited-step inference [36, 16, 45, 67].
• Finally, the coordinate challenge is handled via relevance-preserving corruption, a condition-
adaptive forward process that uses the conditional–unconditional denoising gap to identify in-
formative chart coordinates and reduce their corruption variance early. Unlike auxiliary-prior or
conditioning-annealing approaches [74, 67], we directly modulate corruption covariance, ensuring
class-relevant structure survives into the reverse trajectory where it is most recoverable [38, 59].
Sec. 2 formalizes generation as a support-recovery problem, decoupling geometric validity from
clinical fidelity. We establish theoretical guarantees and provide ablations (App. F) validating our
class-anchored conditioning, amortized bridge, and relevance-preserving corruption. Empirically
(see Sec. 3), MAGNET demonstrates robust conditional fidelity across ABIDE, ADNI, and OASIS-3,
achieving superior zero-shot subclass recovery alongside accelerated sampling times.

Methodology
We construct MAGNET using four coupled components, targeting the three challenges identified in
Sec. 1 - label ambiguity from coarse supervision, missing global context under few-step inference,
and coordinate-space corruption in the nonlinear chart. Sec. 2.1–2.2 establish the validity-preserving
chart and ROI-level graph denoiser; Sec. 2.3.1–2.3.3 introduce the three controllers that address label
ambiguity, missing global context, and coordinate-space corruption respectively. Sec. 3.2 evaluates
zero-shot subclass preservation. All proofs, ablations, and geometric analyses are in App. B–H.

MAGNET Framework
2.3.1
Class-anchored conditioning
First problem arises due to coarse labels, where the application of one label to multiple latent
structural subpopulations [76, 82, 81] results in sample points located in different off-axis sub-
regions rather than a single prototype (Fig. 2). Conditional on such labels, generator has no choice
but to create a mean representation for each mode within the class [31], generating geometrically
valid yet clinically unrealistic samples [7, 64, 56]. Existing works on subpopulation identification
and disease heterogeneity modeling shows that an explicit decomposition of the coarse labels into
latent substructures significantly alleviates mode-averaging [76, 82, 81]. Ignoring this sub-manifold
structure harms generalization, analogous to label conflation in classification [39, 12], and biases the
denoiser toward majority modes, compressing diversity toward dominant structural patterns [13, 42].

diagonal-covariance Gaussian mixture in standardized chart coordinates via expectation maximiza-

2.3.2
Amortized structural bridge
Subtype conditioning is a solution for the problem of labeling, but it does not have the required global
structural knowledge to use a few-step reverse sampling technique, hence creating a dilemma for
the choice of either out-of-distribution averaging [7] or constrained manifold learning [67, 45, 16].
In order to resolve this ambiguity without additional forward samples, we condense a structural
feature of the subtype from a pre-trained dictionary of data into an amortized predictor [35, 15].
Unlike self-conditioning, which recycles recursive denoiser outputs [15], this bridge provides an
independently supervised, compact structural prior to offset the limitations of the noisy input xt.

subtype mean or two principal-split means, depending on training support. Given a clean latent x0,

2.3.3
Relevance-preserving corruption
The third failure mode is geometric. Although the chart χ guarantees validity after decoding,
isotropic corruption in chart coordinates is not geometry-neutral: because χ is nonlinear, equal-
variance perturbations can move the noisy state away from the local class-conditional support
that the reverse process must recover. This is especially harmful in the few-step regime where
interpolation steps are larger. Motivated by geometry-aware diffusion and on-manifold correction
methods [38, 8, 33, 47, 59, 65], we therefore replace isotropic corruption with a manifold-faithful,
condition-adaptive noising rule as trajectory deviation at any timestep leads to accumulation of
deviations away from the manifold [65, 59].

3
Experimental Results
We evaluate MAGNET on three neuroimaging cohorts: ABIDE [27], ADNI [80], and OASIS-3 [46].
Regional time series are extracted using Nilearn [19] with the MSDL atlas [77] (d = 39), z-scored,
and converted to correlation matrices via OAS-estimated covariances [22]. The dataset comprises
4989 scans with strict subject-level splits: ABIDE (846 scans; 455 control, 391 positive), ADNI
(2343 scans; 980 control, 1363 positive), and OASIS-3 (1800 scans; 1047 control, 753 positive).
In the primary benchmark, we use binary labels (control = 0, all diagnoses = 1), intentionally imposing
coarse labels to test recovery of within-class structure (Sec. 3.1). A separate zero-shot multiclass
evaluation assesses whether finer clinical subtypes are preserved despite binary training (Sec. 3.2).
Baseline details and experimental setup are provided in App. C and App. D respectively.
3.1
Main Benchmark
Under matched sampling budgets, MAGNET improves upon prior baselines, delivering up to a 21%
relative increase in class-conditional fidelity (α, β-F1) over the strongest validity-preserving baselines
(Tab. 1). These gains are most pronounced on heterogeneous cohorts: 21% on OASIS-3 and 7% on
ADNI. This suggests that MAGNET better captures the within-class clinical variation that confounds
support recovery in these cohorts. Furthermore, this strict geometric fidelity directly translates to
downstream utility: MAGNET achieves the highest or tied-best classifier-based F1 scores across all
three cohorts. Crucially, MAGNET establishes this performance while simultaneously accelerating
generation. Computational graph reparameterizations have similarly been explored to improve the
efficiency of probabilistic deep learning [57]. The primary speedup over flow-matching baselines
arises from the graph-denoiser architecture operating in discrete diffusion steps rather than continuous
ODE integration; the amortized structural bridge provides an additional approximately 25 −32×
reduction relative to teacher-bridge alternatives (Tab. 5).
Fig. 4 further confirms these gains topologically by visualizing the spatial mismatch of cortical
hubs relative to real test scans. While baselines exhibit brighter surface regions indicative of larger
spatial deviations, MAGNET generates noticeably darker surfaces that closely resemble the real
distributions, yielding the lowest hub errors across all three cohorts. Finally, we note that generic
discrete graph models (e.g., GDSS) fail to achieve this level of fidelity. Because these models are
natively designed for sparse networks with discrete edge attributes, they cannot inherently enforce
the strict positive-definite and unit-diagonal constraints of correlation matrices. Consequently, their
reliance on post-hoc projection to force outputs into valid dense, continuous functional connectomes
severely disrupts the generative trajectory and destroys subtle structural patterns, rendering them
fundamentally insufficient for conditional support recovery (see App. C for detailed analysis).
3.2
Zero-Shot Recovery of Fine-Grained Clinical Subclasses
To test if binary-trained generators natively preserve unseen fine-grained clinical structure, we map
generated samples into these subclasses using a classifier trained exclusively on real data. Because
no generator is exposed to these labels during training, this zero-shot setting isolates the preservation
of classifier-resolved clinical organization rather than validating biological taxonomy. This confound
is controlled across methods by applying the same real-data-trained resolver to every generator.

Distributional alignment has also been studied through optimal transport regularization [52]. Results
indicate MAGNET better preserves fine-grained clinical organization under coarse binary supervision.

evidence of subclass preservation. In stark contrast, the generic discrete graph baseline (GDSS-proj)
suffers catastrophic degradation in this fine-grained setting (e.g., dropping to zero F1 on OASIS-

necessary for resolving unseen clinical subclasses (see App. C for details).
3.3
Graph Backbone and Controller Necessity
MAGNET relies on two core pillars: a validity-preserving chart for mathematical feasibility [18] and a
graph-structured denoiser for relational inductive biases [22, 21, 55]. Tab. 3 isolates these components
under a fixed six-step generation budget [72] to evaluate backbone necessity and controller synergy
in resolving coarse-label deficits.
The structural gains of the graph backbone. Switching from the vector denoiser to the graph back-
bone yields substantial gains across all datasets. While the pullback chart guarantees mathematical
validity, it lacks a mechanism for modeling ROI relationships; the graph denoiser supplies this crucial
inductive bias, bridging mathematical soundness with anatomical relevance [22, 21, 55].
Coupled controller dynamics. The control components: subtype conditioning, amortized bridge,
relevance-preserving noise, and classifier-free guidance [36] act as interdependent latent transforms
rather than strictly additive modules. Individually, the amortized bridge provides the largest im-
provement, particularly on the highly diverse ADNI dataset (α, β-F1 improves from 0.57 to 0.69).

4
Conclusion
Geometric validity is necessary for conditional connectome synthesis, but it is not sufficient for
structural recovery. In this paper, we introduced MAGNET, a structure-aware generator that uses
the normalized-Cholesky chart for validity and focuses the modeling problem on recovering class-
conditional organization inside the valid correlation manifold. MAGNET combines an ROI-level
graph denoiser with class-anchored subtype conditioning, an amortized structural bridge, and
relevance-preserving corruption to reduce ambiguity from coarse labels, global structural context,
and condition-relevant coordinates. Across three cohorts, MAGNET improves distributional fidelity,
support recovery, zero-shot clinical alignment, and few-step sampling efficiency over strong baselines.
MAGNET combines an ROI-level graph denoiser with class-anchored subtype conditioning, an
amortized structural bridge, and relevance-preserving corruption to address ambiguity from coarse
labels, missing global context, and corruption of condition-relevant coordinates. Future directions
include multi-modal imaging inputs, longitudinal trajectory generation, and evaluation of synthetic
cohort utility in downstream clinical prediction. More broadly, the amortized bridge principle of dis-
tilling structural summaries into fast inference-time predictors could be extended to other constrained
generative settings beyond connectomes.

Although our amortized bridge is trained against a teacher target (drawing directly on the principles
of knowledge distillation [35]), our structural objective is entirely different. In traditional distillation,
the capacity is shrunk in the same output space, while we use the structure for addressing a lack
of context, to provide a macroscopic overview of the conditional probability distribution which
cannot be captured by an isolated state Xt via few-step sampling [36, 15]. Equation 8 represents
this difference by constraining the amortization tracking error rather than shrinking the capacity. As
shown in Appendix F.1, tracking the teacher target effectively leads to increased generative accuracy,
allowing us to maintain structural properties of the teacher bridge with up to approximately 25 −32×
savings in sampling cost.

C
Baselines
We compare MAGNET against baselines designed to isolate the roles of validity-preserving geometry,
learned transport, and structure-aware conditional recovery. All baselines are evaluated on the same
splits, preprocessing pipeline, and sampling budgets as MAGNET, so differences reflect model design
rather than data handling or integration depth.
DiffeoCFM. DiffeoCFM [18] is the strongest prior validity-preserving baseline in our setting. It
performs conditional flow matching in a global pullback chart of the correlation manifold induced by
the normalized-Cholesky parameterization. This baseline tests whether validity-preserving transport
alone is sufficient for conditional connectome generation.
DiffeoGauss. DiffeoGauss is a structural ablation of DiffeoCFM in which the learned transport
dynamics are replaced by a class-conditional Gaussian model fit directly in the same pullback chart. It
therefore preserves the geometry of DiffeoCFM while removing learned flow dynamics. Comparing
against DiffeoGauss isolates the contribution of learned transport beyond the validity-preserving
coordinate system itself.
TriangCFM. TriangCFM is a Euclidean flow-matching baseline defined directly on the strict lower-
triangular coordinates, without a validity-preserving correlation-manifold chart. Generated samples
must therefore be mapped back to the correlation manifold through post-hoc projection. This baseline
isolates the importance of validity-preserving geometry by retaining continuous transport while
removing the pullback construction.
Real Data. The real-data row is not a generative model, but an empirical oracle computed by
comparing disjoint real-data splits under the same evaluation pipeline. It quantifies the finite-sample
ceiling induced by split-wise estimation and provides a reference point for interpreting generative
fidelity and downstream utility.
Why not generic graph generative baselines? We do not include graph generative models such as
GDSS [40] or DiGress [79] because they are designed for sparse graphs with discrete edge labels,
while our task requires the generation of dense, continuous-valued functional connectomes. In
functional connectomes, every ROI pair carries a real-valued dependency, and the output must satisfy
symmetry and unit-diagonal structure. These requirements fall outside the design assumptions of such
baselines. Adapting them to this setting would require a nontrivial redesign of their representation,
denoising objective, and output parameterization, along with additional procedures to force a valid
connectome structure. This would make the comparison hard to interpret, since any performance
degradation could come either from the underlying model or from the adaptation stack needed to fit a
fundamentally different data type. We therefore focus on baselines that are inherently designed for
continuous connectome generation under comparable structural assumptions.
To make this limitation concrete, we also implemented an adaptation of GDSS. We trained the
official GDSS adjacency score network on dense signed ROI adjacency matrices and applied post-
hoc projection to force each generated output back to the correlation-matrix domain. We denote
this adapted baseline as GDSS-proj. This is not a native connectome generator: the projection
step is required because generic graph diffusion does not enforce positive definiteness or unit-
diagonal correlation structure. The results in Tables 1–2 support the design choice behind MAGNET.
Although GDSS-proj is fast, it substantially underperforms validity-aware connectome generators on
support fidelity and zero-shot subclass recovery, indicating that generic graph diffusion plus post-hoc
projection is insufficient for dense functional connectome synthesis.

held-out support) and β-recall (coverage of that support), summarizing the trade-off with their
harmonic mean, α, β-F1 [1]. Because generative fidelity alone does not guarantee the preservation of
task-relevant structures, we also evaluate downstream utility by training a logistic regression classifier
on Riemannian tangent-space features extracted from generated samples, reporting ROC-AUC and
F1 on real validation connectomes.

Training and sampling protocol. All methods are evaluated across 10 disjoint train/validation

Architectural Details
MAGNET couples the validity-preserving normalized-Cholesky chart with a region-aware denoising
backbone. In the MSDL setting, each connectome has 39 regions, giving 741 chart coordinates.
Rather than processing the noisy latent state xt as a flat vector, the denoiser first lifts it into a
symmetric ROI-level matrix A(xt). The chart coordinates are written into the strict lower triangle,
reflected across the diagonal, and the diagonal is set to zero. The ith row, denoted ai(xt), is then
interpreted as the current noisy connectivity profile of region i.
Each region is represented as one token. The initial token combines the row-wise connectivity profile
with anatomical identity and global conditioning: h(0)
i
= Winai(xt)+pi +ct. Here, Win is a learned
row projection, pi is a learned ROI positional embedding, and ct is a shared conditioning broadcast to
all regions. The conditioning vector combines the diffusion timestep embedding, the class-anchored
subtype embedding, and the amortized structural bridge embedding. In the implementation, the
bridge embedding is passed through a learned gate before being injected, allowing the denoiser to
modulate how strongly the global structural summary affects each reverse step.
The token sequence is processed by a pre-norm Transformer encoder with hidden size 128, four
layers, four QK-normalized attention heads, GeGLU feed-forward blocks of width 512, and dropout
0.1. Because attention is applied across all ROI tokens, each region updates its representation using
information from the full current connectome. This gives the denoiser a relational inductive bias
without hard-coding an anatomical edge graph. The learned positional embeddings preserve ROI
identity, so the model remains tied to the atlas-defined ordering of brain regions.
After the transformer stack, MAGNET maps the final token matrix H(L) back to chart coordinates.
A row-wise output projection produces a square matrix R, which is symmetrized and vectorized over
the strict lower triangle: E = 1

target in the same normalized-Cholesky chart used by the sampler. The chart itself, rather than the
denoiser output alone, is what guarantees that decoded samples are valid correlation matrices.

F.1
Amortized Structural Bridge
To evaluate the role of the amortized structural bridge, we compare six variants: no bridge, self-
conditioned bridge, teacher bridge at inference, amortized bridge without Lbridge, amortized bridge
with Lbridge, and the full MAGNET objective. For bridge-enabled variants, we report the Bridge
Distillation Error ∥Bψ(xt, t, s) −b⋆(x0, s, t)∥2
2, which measures how well the amortized bridge
tracks the time-dependent teacher target.
Table 5 shows a clear bridge-quality versus structural-fidelity pattern. Lower distillation error is
consistently associated with stronger α, β-F1, while removing Lbridge causes a severe bridge-tracking
failure without yielding a corresponding fidelity gain. This is the regime the bridge is designed for,
and the ablation matches the Remark of proposition 2: the amortized bridge is useful to the extent
that it tracks the teacher target well.
The efficiency gap is equally important. Teacher-bridge and self-conditioned alternatives are sub-
stantially slower because they require a second structural pass at inference, whereas the amortized
bridge preserves the few-step sampling regime. In practice, the bridge-enabled MAGNET variants
retain roughly 30 −60× lower sampling cost than the teacher and self-conditioned alternatives while
delivering the strongest overall fidelity profile. Taken together, these results justify the amortized
bridge as the primary structural-conditioning mechanism in MAGNET. It provides the gains of
explicit structural guidance without sacrificing the speed advantages of few-step generation.

F.2
Relevance-Preserving Corruption Ablation
Our relevance-preserving corruption relies on a monotonically decreasing protection schedule, ρ(t),
to shield condition-defining coordinates during early forward diffusion. To evaluate the framework’s
sensitivity to this mechanism, we parameterize the decay curve as ρ(t) = (1 −τt)p, where τt ∈[0, 1]
is the normalized continuous diffusion time (i.e., τt = t/T). The hyperparameter p strictly controls
the temporal density of the protection, dictating how quickly structural coordinates yield to Gaussian
noise, rather than the total magnitude of protection applied.
We ablate this schedule across four distinct regimes: (i) Isotropic (Baseline): No adaptive manifold
preservation; (ii) Low Density (p = 1): Gradual, linear relaxation of constraints; (iii) Adaptive
(p = 3, Ours): Our primary, balanced decay schedule; and (iv) High Density (p = 5): Aggressive,
early release of structural constraints.
Table 6 highlights a clear trend across ABIDE, ADNI, and OASIS-3: MAGNET is robust to the
exact shape of the decay curve. Generative fidelity (α, β-F1) and downstream utility (CAS) remain
consistently stable across the Low, Adaptive, and High density regimes. The adaptive schedule
provides consistent improvement over isotropic diffusion across all three cohorts. Crucially, the
robustness to the exact decay shape (p = 1, 3, 5) confirms that the mechanism is not sensitive
to hyperparameter tuning — any protection schedule that shields condition-relevant coordinates
during the high-noise phase suffices. The primary contribution of relevance-preserving corruption
is providing a cleaner latent trajectory for the bridge and guidance components to operate on, as
evidenced by the full-system results in Table 3.
F.3
Classifier-Free Guidance Scale
The classifier-free guidance scale γ controls how strongly reverse-time sampling is steered toward the
conditional branch. In MAGNET, guidance is not implemented as plain linear extrapolation. Instead,
23


the sampler scales the conditional steering direction while retaining an orthogonal residual from the
unconditional prediction. Figure 5 shows that performance is strongest in the moderate-guidance
regime.
On ADNI, γ = 1.5 gives the best overall fidelity–utility trade-off, improving clearly over both
weak guidance (γ = 0.5) and overly strong guidance (γ ≥2.5). The main failure mode at high
guidance is not precision but coverage: α-precision increases, but β-recall drops sharply, indicating
overspecialization to a narrower subset of the class manifold. OASIS-3 follows the same pattern,
though less sharply, with γ = 1.5 again near-optimal. ABIDE is less sensitive once guidance is
enabled, consistent with its simpler binary structure.
A useful implementation detail is that γ = 1.0 in Fig. 5 is implemented as a discrete branch skip (the
unconditional forward pass is omitted entirely), rather than as a continuous evaluation of the guidance
formula at that scale. Consequently, the transition from γ = 1.0 to γ = 0.5 represents a qualitative
mode change, not a smooth interpolation, which accounts for the performance gap observed at that
boundary. This explains both its lower runtime and its weaker fidelity relative to the moderately
guided regime. We therefore use γ = 1.5 throughout the paper.

F.5
Self-conditioning probability
We also tested the fallback self-conditioning probability psc, which in earlier non-amortized variants
controls how often a first-pass denoiser estimate is reused as auxiliary structural context during
training. In the final MAGNET configuration, however, structural context is supplied primarily by the
amortized bridge, so this fallback mechanism is no longer central to the active model.
Empirically, varying psc from 0.2 to 0.8 produces no meaningful change in generative fidelity or
downstream performance across ABIDE, ADNI, and OASIS-3: all three datasets remain effectively
invariant at the precision reported in the main benchmark, with only minor variation in runtime. We
therefore treat psc as a secondary implementation parameter and use psc = 0.5 throughout the paper.
This confirms that, once the amortized bridge is active, structural recovery is largely insensitive to the
scheduling of the fallback self-conditioning branch.

F.6
Training and Sampling Efficiency
MAGNET trades additional training cost for substantially faster repeated sampling. On a single
NVIDIA A6000 GPU, training requires 532.08 ± 16.66 s per split on ABIDE, 1593.63 ± 27.99 s
on ADNI, and 1242.53 ± 18.49 s on OASIS-3, corresponding to approximately 9–27 minutes per
trained split. This is roughly an order of magnitude more training time than the DiffeoCFM and
TriangCFM baselines under the same hardware setting.
The inference regime is substantially different. MAGNET uses a six-step DDIM sampler and requires
0.38 s, 0.86 s, and 0.63 s per generated split on ABIDE, ADNI, and OASIS-3, respectively, compared
with 9.04 s, 36.08 s, and 23.24 s for DiffeoCFM (Table 1). This corresponds to approximately 24×,
42×, and 37× faster sampling, respectively. The additional training cost is therefore incurred once
when fitting a checkpoint, whereas the sampling advantage is retained whenever that checkpoint is
subsequently used for synthetic cohort generation.
Importantly, this efficiency does not arise from sacrificing conditional fidelity: MAGNET simul-
taneously improves α, β-F1 by 7–21% over the strongest validity-preserving baseline across the
primary cohorts and achieves the lowest zero-shot subclass covariance error in Table 2. These results
characterize the computational trade-off of MAGNET as higher one-time training cost in exchange
for improved support recovery and substantially lower repeated inference cost.
F.7
Sensitivity to λbridge and λspec
The full MAGNET objective combines the diffusion loss with bridge supervision and structural
regularization, L = Ldiff + λbridgeLbridge + λspecLspec. We therefore evaluate whether the gains
of the proposed framework depend on narrowly tuned auxiliary-loss weights. For each loss, we
vary its coefficient over {0, 0.025, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.4} while holding the other coefficient fixed at its
default value of 0.1. The nonzero sweep spans a 16× range around the default operating regime. All
remaining training, sampling, and evaluation settings are unchanged.
Table 7 reveals two complementary properties. First, both auxiliary objectives make a material
contribution to support recovery. Removing the spectral term decreases α, β-F1 from 0.68 to 0.51 on
ABIDE, from 0.76 to 0.68 on ADNI, and from 0.57 to 0.49 on OASIS-3, corresponding to relative
reductions of approximately 11–25%. Removing bridge supervision produces similarly consistent
degradation, reducing α, β-F1 to 0.53, 0.65, and 0.42, respectively. These ablations are consistent
with the intended roles of the two losses: Lbridge constrains the amortized predictor to track its
structural target, while Lspec encourages the denoised latent to preserve global structural statistics.
Second, once either loss is enabled, performance is insensitive to its precise weighting. Across the
nonzero λspec sweep, α, β-F1 varies by at most 0.02 on each cohort; across the nonzero λbridge
sweep, the maximum variation is 0.03. These changes are smaller than, or comparable to, the
split-to-split variability reported in the main benchmark. ROC-AUC and downstream F1 exhibit the
same qualitative stability. This behavior is also consistent with the construction of the objective:
Lbridge is restricted to conditioned examples and weighted by ¯αt, while Lspec is mean-reduced over
the minibatch.
Thus, the auxiliary objectives are important through their presence rather than through a narrowly
selected coefficient. The default choice λbridge = λspec = 0.1 lies within a broad stable region across
all three cohorts, indicating that MAGNET’s performance is not dependent on fine-grained tuning of
either loss weight.

Log-Euclidean representation.
Since C⌈is not a vector space, euclidean operations in matrix space
do not capture the geometry of connectomes faithfully. Standard operations on C1, C2 ∈C⌈treat the
objects as points in a flat vector space, ignoring the intrinsic geometric structure of the set. Hence,
we adopt the log-euclidean representation [3, 4] and define Φ : C⌈→Sym(d),
Φ(C) = log C

Broader Impacts
Conditional connectome synthesis lies at the intersection of two broader challenges: generating valid
samples on constrained manifolds and recovering class-conditional structure under coarse supervision.
MAGNET addresses both, with implications beyond neuroimaging.
On the positive side, generating geometrically valid, class-conditional connectomes supports data-
scarce clinical research by augmenting underrepresented groups, enabling multi-site studies, and
facilitating privacy-preserving data sharing. By modeling within-class heterogeneity via subtype
conditioning, MAGNET avoids a key failure of binary-supervised generators, producing unrealistic
averaged samples, thereby improving the reliability of synthetic augmentation. More broadly,
the work highlights a general problem: class-conditional manifold awareness. In domains where

continual learning, and potential extensions to mitigating hallucinations and poor-quality synthesis.
