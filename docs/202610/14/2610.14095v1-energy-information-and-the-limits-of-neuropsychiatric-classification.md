## 摘要
重要性：尽管已有数十年研究，寻找可靠的精神障碍神经影像生物标志物仍未能实现。为什么具有临床后果的疾病在神经影像上仅表现出较弱的可分离性，仍未得到解答。借鉴统计热力学和神经编码的代谢成本，我们提出，精神障碍对大部分共享的脑活动模式库产生受限形变，从而限制了可用于分类的类别特异性信息。  观察结果：我们建立了一个框架，将物理资源约束、景观形变、分布分离与可达到的分类性能联系起来，并利用人类神经影像、合成数据和一个精确指定的物理模型考察其各组成部分。在来自1952名FOR2107参与者（823例重性抑郁障碍；1129名健康对照）的静息态fMRI中，留出样本的能量景观估计与非线性分类器在13项比较中一致（r = 0.996）。在FOR2107的两个中心以及涵盖多种精神科诊断的900名SRPBS参与者中，也出现了类似结果。在合成数据中，分类性能随分布分离度提高而改善，并接近最优。在一个平衡态三势阱模型中，统计能量等于按逆温度缩放的物理势能。适度的形变重新分配了状态占据，而最优快照分类仍然较弱。一项文献图谱发现，现有证据的可比性不足以检验人类的代谢约束。  结论：具有临床后果的差异可能产生于一个大部分共享的神经活动模式库之中。该热力学框架在特定假设下将物理形变与分类联系起来；其在人类中的代谢实现仍有待检验。检验这一机制需要对脑活动、躯体生理和代谢进行联合测量。

## Abstract
Importance: The search for reliable neuroimaging biomarkers of mental disorders has remained elusive despite decades of research. Why clinically consequential disorders yield weak neuroimaging separability remains unresolved. Drawing on statistical thermodynamics and the metabolic costs of neural coding, we propose that psychiatric disorders produce constrained deformations of a largely shared repertoire of brain activity patterns, limiting the class-specific information available for classification.   Observations: We develop a framework linking physical resource constraints, landscape deformation, distributional separation, and attainable classification, and examine its components using human neuroimaging, synthetic data, and an exactly specified physical model. In resting-state fMRI from 1952 FOR2107 participants (823 with major depressive disorder; 1129 healthy controls), held-out energy-landscape estimates and nonlinear classifiers agreed across 13 comparisons (r = 0.996). Similar results emerged at both FOR2107 centers and in 900 SRPBS participants spanning multiple psychiatric diagnoses. In synthetic data, classification improved with distributional separation and approached the optimum. In an equilibrium three-well model, statistical energy equaled physical potential energy scaled by inverse temperature. Modest deformation redistributed state occupancy while optimal snapshot classification remained weak. A literature map found insufficiently comparable evidence to test the human metabolic constraint.   Conclusions: Clinically consequential differences may arise within a largely shared neural repertoire. The thermodynamic framework connects physical deformation to classification under specified assumptions; its metabolic implementation in humans remains untested. Testing this mechanism requires joint measurements of brain activity, bodily physiology, and metabolism.
