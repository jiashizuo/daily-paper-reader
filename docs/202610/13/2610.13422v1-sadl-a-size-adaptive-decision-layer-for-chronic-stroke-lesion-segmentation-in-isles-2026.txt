# SADL: A Size-Adaptive Decision Layer for Chronic Stroke Lesion Segmentation in ISLES 2026 

Marek Wodzinski 1,2[0000 −0002 −8076 −6246] , Gniewosz Drwiega 1[0000 −0002 −1968 −2238] , and Wojciech Szymanski 1,2[0009 −0001 −2043 −9104]

Abstract. Chronic ischemic stroke lesions are difficult to segment on T1-weighted MRI. Their appearance overlaps with healthy tissue, and their volumes span four orders of magnitude, so voxel-level and lesion-level metrics often disagree. We build an eleven-member nnU-Net ensem-ble that covers five design axes: a baseline dice + top-K loss, heavy aug-mentation, per-lesion-volume loss re-weighting, stochastic weight aver-aging, and a sampling-and-architecture block that pairs cohort-balanced sampling with a residual-encoder backbone. Weighted soft-map fusion of the members feeds a tuned decision layer, SADL , that estimates lesion volume, applies an adaptive per-lesion-size threshold, prunes components by voxel count and peak probability, and adds a never-empty rescue. All fusion weights and per-bucket parameters are fitted on out-of-fold (OOF) predictions, so tuning never touches the held-out test set. On a site-disjoint 91-case holdout, SADL reaches Dice 0.7322, lesion-F1 0.7461 and PR-AUC 0.8430 when scored offline with the challenge’s released evaluation code. A paired non-parametric bootstrap against a reference single ensemble member confirms the lesion-F1 gain at p=0 .024 , and the gain is largest in the small-lesion regime, where the rescue step recovers cases that a fixed threshold would silently discard. 

Keywords: Chronic stroke · Lesion segmentation · T1-weighted MRI · Ensemble learning · nnU-Net · Decision-layer post-processing · ATLAS · ISLES 2026. 

## 1 Introduction 

Chronic stroke lesion segmentation on T1-weighted MRI feeds directly into out-come prediction, neuro-restorative studies, and post-stroke rehabilitation plan-ning. Manual delineation is slow and rater-variable, and the task itself is gen-uinely hard: chronic lesions look very close to normal tissue, their volumes span from a fraction of a millilitre to several hundred, and public cohorts are pooled across scanners with different acquisition protocols. In that regime, voxel-level arXiv:2610.13422v1 [cs.CV] 9 Oct 2026 2 M. Wodzinski et al. 

metrics such as Dice can overstate performance on cases whose small lesions dominate lesion-wise recognition [6]. Since the release of ATLAS v2, most published models for this task have been variations around nnU-Net [2,3] and its residual-encoder update. Once the backbone plateaus around Dice 0.72 on ATLAS-derived holdouts, further gains typically come from three levers: training-side variants (loss re-weighting, heavier augmentation, weight-space averaging, cohort balancing), architectural diversity (residual and transformer-hybrid encoders), and post-hoc calibration of the decision layer that turns soft ensembles into binary masks. This paper works on the third lever. We assemble an eleven-member nnU-Net ensemble that covers five design axes and, on top of a weighted soft-map fusion of its outputs, add a decision layer we call SADL . The layer estimates lesion volume, applies an adaptive per-lesion-volume threshold, prunes connected components, and adds a never-empty rescue. Every fusion weight and per-bucket parameter is fitted on out-of-fold predictions inside a 5-fold cross-validation, so the 91-case site-disjoint holdout is only used for reporting. Under the ISLES 2026 released evaluation code [6], the tuned layer beats the Dice-strongest single ensemble member (BucketWeighted, vref = 3 mL) by +0 .6 pp Dice and +5 .4 pp lesion-F1 overall, and by +7 .4 pp lesion-F1 on the < 0.5 mL bucket; a paired bootstrap against a separate reference member ( vref = 5 mL) gives a lesion-F1 lift of 

+2 .9 pp at p=0 .024 , and the improvement concentrates in the small-lesion regime where the rescue step earns its keep. 

## 2 Methods 

2.1 Dataset and splits 

Training data comes from ATLAS v2.0 [7] together with the Stroke Outcome Optimization Project (SOOP, 169 sessions) [1], aggregated and released through ISLES 2026 [4]: 1,453 T1-weighted sessions across 55 sites, each with a manually delineated binary lesion mask. The pool is built from three main cohorts (Train-ing: 653 sessions; ATLAS3, which subsumes SOOP: 496; Testing: 299) plus a residual of five sessions with missing or auxiliary cohort labels, absorbed into the main cohorts using their modal label. We reserve three sites (R018, R027, R047) as a site-disjoint 91-case holdout that is large enough for a per-lesion-size analysis while still fitting the CPU budget of our decision-layer tuner, and we run 5-fold cross-validation on the remaining 1,362 sessions. All models are trained in nnU-Net’s plans space at 1 mm isotropic; predictions are resampled back to native T1w geometry before scoring. 

2.2 Backbone 

Every member of our pool is a subclass of nnU-Net v2.7.0 [2]. The upstream codebase is kept unmodified and all our extensions live in a thin wrapper package, so training and inference follow nnU-Net’s established recipe (deep supervision, SADL for ISLES 2026 3

Gaussian sliding-window inference, mirror-based test-time augmentation) unless a specific member deliberately overrides it. Two encoder families appear in the pool: the default PlainConvUNet, and the residual-encoder variant from the “nnU-Net Revisited” update [3]. 

2.3 Ensemble pool 

The eleven members span five design axes chosen to inject complementary er-ror modes: a baseline (dice-plus-top-K cross-entropy); an augmentation member (DA5 heavy schedule, 700 epochs); a loss re-weighting axis ( BucketWeighted )with per-sample weight wi = clip( vref / max( vi, ε ), 0.5, 4.0) at three reference vol-umes vref ∈ { 2, 3, 5} mL; weight-space averaging (SWA [5] from epoch 350, com-bined with the loss re-weighting); and a sampling-and-architecture block gath-ering cohort-balanced sampling, curriculum-by-lesion-size, a cohort-conditioned mixture-of-experts head, synthetic-lesion diffusion augmentation, and the Res-Enc backbone. All eleven members are trained independently on the standard nnU-Net 5-fold split of the training pool, and every fold’s raw soft-max is written to disk so that the decision layer can be tuned offline.

The tunable parameters ( wm, τB , nB , per-CC gates) are all fitted per fold on the OOF validation set: weights by coordinate ascent on the simplex against a Dice-plus-lesion-F1 composite, and per-bucket parameters by a coarse grid followed by coordinate ascent. The tuner runs in minutes on a modest CPU allocation, so we re-tune whenever a new member joins the pool. The fitted thresholds sit around 0.29 on the two small-lesion buckets and 0.41 on the mid and large buckets: the small-lesion gate is deliberately more permissive to compensate for the ensemble’s under-confidence there.

## 3 Results 

Table 1 compares the tuned SADL ensemble against a curated subset of the eleven-member pool and against the uniform-mean-fusion baseline. The table keeps one row per ensemble axis rather than listing every member, since the axes motivate the pool and the individual differences within an axis (for example, the three reference-volume variants of the volume-weighted loss) are of secondary interest here. SADL tops every metric except absolute volume difference. Against the Dice-strongest single member (BucketWeighted with vref = 3 mL) the gain is +0 .62 pp Dice and +5 .4 pp lesion-F1; against the uniform-mean ensemble the gain is 

+1 .03 pp Dice, +6 .1 pp lesion-F1, and +0 .14 pp PR-AUC. The absolute lesion count difference also drops from 0.98 to 0.86 , or about eleven fewer spurious components across the 91-case holdout. The uniform-mean ensemble averages the eleven soft maps and thresholds them at 0.5 before a modest per-bucket clean-up, and it gains only +0 .5 pp Dice over the vanilla dice + top-K baseline. Almost the whole SADL gain therefore comes from the decision layer, not from adding one more ensemble member. Table 2 slices the same 91 cases into four size buckets and reports both Dice and lesion-F1. Two things stand out. SADL wins three of the four buckets on Dice; on the 0.5–5 mL bucket BucketWeighted ( vref = 3 mL) retains a small edge (0.596 vs 0.579). SADL’s margin over that BucketWeighted variant is small on ≥ 50 mL cases (0.877 vs 0.874) and largest on < 0.5 mL cases ( +4 .4 pp). Lesion-F1 is where the decision layer really earns its keep: at < 0.5 mL SADL scores 0.767 , about 7.4 pp above the strongest single-model config and 17 .4 pp above the uniform-mean ensemble. ## 4 Discussion and Conclusion 

A well-tuned decision layer is a low-cost gain on top of uniform mean fusion of a mature nnU-Net ensemble. SADL adds no new training run and no new member: it re-uses the soft-max outputs already on disk, and because its parameters are fitted on OOF predictions the tuner is cheap to re-run whenever a new member joins the pool. Two caveats: our 91-case site-disjoint holdout is not the hid-den challenge cohort, and not every pool strategy paid off (diffusion-augmented synthesis ranked last despite promising training-time Dice). The three ideas to carry over are the size-bucket-conditioned threshold, the never-empty rescue on the handful of < 0.5 mL cases per fold, and the OOF-fitted fusion weights. The tuned policy is a small JSON blob that any team running an nnU-Net ensemble can drop on top of their own fusion, and it drives our submission container as-is.
