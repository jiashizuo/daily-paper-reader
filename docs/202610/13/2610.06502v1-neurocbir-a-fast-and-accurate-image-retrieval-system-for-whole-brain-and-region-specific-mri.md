## 摘要
神经影像学中基于内容的图像检索（CBIR）能够识别结构相似的大脑扫描，支持诊断、预后和治疗规划；然而，现有方法通常受限于小规模数据集、单一脑区或粗粒度类别标签，从而限制了其临床实用性和泛化能力。在此，我们提出 NeuroCBIR，一个用于快速灵活检索全脑和特定脑区 3D T1w MRI 扫描的框架。共提取 103 个皮层和皮层下区域，以支持全脑和区域级查询。NeuroCBIR 利用变分自编码器（VAE）结合对比学习学到的潜在表示，生成能够捕捉解剖模式的扫描特异性嵌入。这些嵌入在受试者重识别、零样本年龄预测和零样本多类病理分层中进行了评估。重识别性能在全脑和脑区水平上均很高（前 5 张检索图像的平均精度均值（mAP@5）≥ 98.4%），并在不同数据集和采集条件下具有稳健的泛化能力。尽管 NeuroCBIR 并未针对年龄预测或病理分层进行训练，但这两个任务的零样本评估表明，这些嵌入编码了对下游任务有意义的信息。在 4 核 CPU 上，每次扫描的嵌入提取约需 18.7 秒，而相似性搜索实际上几乎是瞬时的（小于 0.01 秒）。NeuroCBIR 已公开可用，面向脑 MRI，并提供超过 26,000 个预计算的 T1w MRI 嵌入。它支持可重复研究、特定脑区的灵活性以及具有临床意义的个性化诊断支持。该软件可在 https://github.com/minnelab/NeuroCBIR 获取。

## Abstract
Content-based image retrieval (CBIR) in neuroimaging enables the identification of structurally similar brain scans, supporting diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment planning; however, existing methods are often limited to small datasets, single brain regions, or coarse class labels, thereby restricting their clinical utility and generalizability.   Here, we present NeuroCBIR, a framework for fast and flexible retrieval of both whole-brain and region-specific 3D T1w MRI scans. A total of 103 cortical and subcortical regions are extracted to enable both whole-brain and region-level queries.   NeuroCBIR leverages latent representations learned by a variational autoencoder (VAE) combined with contrastive learning, producing scan-specific embeddings that capture anatomical patterns. These embeddings were evaluated for subject re-identification, zero-shot age prediction, and zero-shot multi-class pathology stratification. Re-identification performance was high across both whole-brain and brain-region levels (mean average precision across the top-5 retrieved images (mAP@5) >= 98.4%), with robust generalization across datasets and acquisition conditions. While NeuroCBIR is not trained for age prediction or pathology stratification, zero-shot evaluations for these two tasks demonstrate that the embeddings encode meaningful information for downstream tasks.   Embedding extraction on a 4-core CPU required approximately 18.7 s per scan, whereas similarity search was effectively instantaneous (less than 0.01 s).   NeuroCBIR is publicly available for brain MRI with more than 26,000 precomputed T1w MRI embeddings. It supports reproducible research, region-specific flexibility, and clinically meaningful personalized diagnostic support. The software is available at https://github.com/minnelab/NeuroCBIR.
