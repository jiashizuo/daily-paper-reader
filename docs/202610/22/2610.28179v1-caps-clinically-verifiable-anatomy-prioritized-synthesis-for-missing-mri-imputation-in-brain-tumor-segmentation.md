## 摘要
多参数MRI是神经肿瘤学的金标准，但在真实临床实践中获取完整的四模态协议可能不可行。在处理缺失模态时，现有自动化系统被迫做出关键妥协：稳健的分割器作为不可解释的“黑箱”运行，绕过图像生成；而标准合成模型通常产生模糊重建，缺乏诊断可靠性。为解决该问题，我们提出CAPS，一种临床可验证的解剖优先合成框架，兼顾计算精度与临床透明度。我们的多模态生成架构由混合目标函数（L_1 + L_AFP + L_DualDice）驱动，该函数显式强制保留深层解剖特征和严格拓扑边界，而非仅依赖逐像素优化。在BraTS成人胶质瘤数据集上全面评估，该框架在基于强度的指标上实现了优越的合成保真度，并展现出稳健的下游分割性能（平均Dice 82.38%），成功恢复了可验证的视觉上下文。此外，该框架表现出卓越的零样本跨域泛化能力，在完全未见过的脑膜瘤、儿童和撒哈拉以南非洲队列中无需域特定适应即保持高结构保真度。最后，盲法放射学评估证实，我们的解剖感知方法显著提升了感知图像质量和临床实用性，为临床医生提供可信赖的视觉证据，以信任下游自动化工作流。

## Abstract
Multi-parametric MRI is the gold standard for neuro-oncology, yet acquiring complete 4-modality protocols may be unfeasible in real-world clinical practice. When handling missing modalities, existing automated systems force a critical compromise: robust segmenters operate as uninterpretable "black boxes" that bypass image generation, while standard synthesis models often produce blurry reconstructions lacking diagnostic reliability. To address this issue, we propose CAPS, a Clinically-Verifiable Anatomy-Prioritized Synthesis framework that reconciles computational precision with clinical transparency. Our multi-modal generative architecture is driven by a hybrid objective function (L_1 + L_AFP + L_DualDice) that explicitly enforces the preservation of deep anatomical features and strict topological boundaries, rather than relying solely on pixel-wise optimization. Evaluated comprehensively on the BraTS Adult Glioma dataset, the framework achieves superior synthesis fidelity across intensity-based metrics, and demonstrates robust downstream segmentation performance (82.38% mean Dice), successfully restoring verifiable visual context. Furthermore, the framework exhibits exceptional zero-shot cross-domain generalization, maintaining high structural fidelity across completely unseen Meningioma, Pediatric, and Sub-Saharan African cohorts without domain-specific adaptation. Finally, a blinded radiological assessment confirms that our anatomy-aware approach significantly enhances both perceived image quality and clinical utility, providing clinicians with the reliable visual evidence required to trust downstream automated workflows.
