Abstract —Undersampled magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) reconstruction aims to recover high-quality images from partially acquired k-space data, thereby reducing scan time. However, missing frequency-domain measurements often lead to residual aliasing artifacts, blurred edges, and loss of local structural details. In this paper, we propose AGGR-Mamba, a lightweight output-stage residual refinement method built upon a Mamba backbone for undersampled brain MRI reconstruction. The Mamba backbone first produces an initial reconstruction, and the proposed refinement modules then correct the remaining local errors and high-frequency artifacts in a controlled residual manner.Specifically, an Attention Refine Head is introduced to recalibrate local anatomical structures, edge textures, and residual-error regions through channel and spatial attention. In addition, a GDFN-style gated residual refinement branch is designed to selectively preserve informative residual features while suppressing redundant or unstable responses. By combin-ing attention-based recalibration with gated residual selection, AGGR-Mamba improves local structure recovery without sub-stantially disturbing the backbone reconstruction. Experiments are conducted on the IXI brain MRI dataset with T1- and T2-weighted images under acceleration factors of R = 4 and R = 8 .The results show that AGGR-Mamba achieves competitive PSNR and SSIM performance across different reconstruction settings, demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed output-stage refinement strategy. 

Index Terms —Magnetic resonance image reconstruction; un-dersampling; Mamba; IXI dataset 

I. INTRODUCTION Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is an important medical imaging technique that provides high soft-tissue contrast, rich structural information, and no exposure to ionizing radiation. Owing to these advantages, it has been widely used in the analysis of brain disorders, clinical diagnosis, and medical research [1]. However, MRI data acquisition is typically time-consuming. By acquiring fewer k-space measurements, undersampling can reduce scan time, but the resulting lack of complete frequency-domain information makes reconstructed images susceptible to aliasing artifacts, loss of textural details, and blurred edges [3]. Although conventional compressed sensing MRI methods are supported by a solid theoretical foundation [4], [5], they typically rely on manually designed regularization terms and require the tuning of multiple hyperparameters [6], while also suffering from relatively low computational efficiency [7]. Driven by advances in deep learning [8], [9], CNN-based approaches have been widely explored for learning the recon-struction mapping between undersampled and fully sampled MR images [10]. CNN-based methods are well suited for local feature extraction, enabling effective recovery of edges and textural details. However, their reliance on local convolu-tional operations limits their capacity to capture long-range dependencies and global anatomical structures. GAN-based approaches were later explored to enhance the perceptual arXiv:2610.28291v1 [cs.CV] 20 Oct 2026 quality of reconstructed images [11], [12], but the adversarial learning process may introduce training instability and produce anatomically implausible structures. To better capture global contextual dependencies, Transformer-based approaches have been explored for MRI reconstruction [13]. These methods exploit self-attention to capture long-range dependencies, thereby partially compensating for the inherent limitation of CNNs in global context modeling. However, the self-attention mechanism in Transformers incurs high computational complexity, often resulting in substantial memory footprint and computational burden in high-resolution medical image reconstruction [14]. In recent years, state space models (SSMs), exemplified by Mamba [15], have introduced a new paradigm for long-sequence modeling. Compared with conventional Transformers, Mamba is capable of modeling long-range dependencies at significantly lower complexity and has been progressively integrated into medical image reconstruction tasks. Despite the strengths of Mamba-type reconstruction frame-works in global structural modeling and computational effi-ciency [16], [17], the restoration of local details and edge-structure preservation at the output stage in undersampled MRI reconstruction can still be further improved. On the one hand, the reconstructed images may still retain local residual artifacts and high-frequency errors. On the other hand, fine anatomical edges, texture transition regions, and low-contrast structures are susceptible to blurring or insufficient detail during reconstruction. Moreover, a conventional output Refine Head typically applies a relatively uniform correction across the entire image and struggles to distinguish valid structural details from residual errors and spurious high-frequency noise, which may compromise the stability of the refinement process. To address these limitations, this paper proposes AGGR-Mamba, an output-stage attention-guided and gated residual refinement method for undersampled MRI reconstruction. The proposed method uses a Mamba backbone to obtain an initial reconstruction and attaches a lightweight refinement branch at the output stage. This branch is designed to compensate for local reconstruction errors, edge blurring, and residual high-frequency artifacts that may remain after the backbone recon-struction. The refinement process combines attention-guided local detail recalibration with gated residual feature selection, allowing the network to enhance informative anatomical struc-tures while suppressing unstable residual responses. The major contributions of this study are as follows:  

> •

A lightweight output-stage residual refinement design. 

We introduce a lightweight residual refinement branch after the Mamba backbone. Instead of modifying the main reconstruction process, the proposed design refines the backbone output by compensating for local errors and residual artifacts, leading to improved local structural representation in the reconstructed image.  

> •

An attention-guided local detail recalibration mecha-nism. The proposed Attention Refine Head uses channel and spatial attention to adaptively recalibrate channel and spatial responses associated with local structures and residual-error regions. This design reduces uniform correction over the entire image and makes the refinement process more focused on structurally informative areas.  

> •

A gated residual feature selection strategy. Since residual features may contain useful high-frequency de-tails as well as noise and unstable responses, we in-troduce a GDFN-style gated branch to selectively filter candidate residual features. This mechanism enhances reconstruction-relevant details while suppressing ineffec-tive high-frequency noise and residual artifacts. II. R ELATED WORK 

A. CNN-based MRI Reconstruction 

CNN-based MRI reconstruction methods generally learn a nonlinear mapping from undersampled images to their fully sampled counterparts. Representative architectures include U-Net, deep residual networks, and cascaded convolutional net-works [18], [19]. These methods are effective at extract-ing local features and can recover edges, textures, and fine structural details. However, because convolutional operations mainly rely on local receptive fields, CNN-based models may struggle to restore subtle details and delineate structural boundaries clearly, especially in complex brain anatomy and highly accelerated undersampling scenarios. 

B. Physics-guided MRI Reconstruction 

Physics-guided MRI reconstruction methods embed the imaging model of MRI into deep reconstruction networks [20]. These methods commonly rely on a data consistency module to keep the reconstructed result consistent with the sampled k-space data. By combining physical constraints with the representational capacity of deep networks, these methods tend to provide more stable performance in undersampled MRI reconstruction. Models such as E2E-VarNet [21] fall into this category. The Mamba backbone used in this study follows the same principle of physics-guided reconstruction, where the backbone reconstruction module and data consistency con-straint jointly contribute to improved reconstruction quality. 

C. Transformer-based MRI Reconstruction 

Transformer-based methods have also been explored for MRI reconstruction, mainly because self-attention can model long-range dependencies beyond the local receptive field of CNNs. For example, ReconFormer [22] adopts a recurrent Transformer design with multi-scale attention to improve reconstruction quality. Despite these advantages, the self-attention operation is often costly, and its use in high-resolution image reconstruction can result in heavy GPU memory usage and increased computational burden [23]. 

D. Mamba-based MRI Reconstruction 

Mamba-based methods model long-range dependencies through state space models. Compared with Transformer-based models, their linear-complexity sequence modeling is more efficient for long inputs, which is beneficial for high-resolution medical image reconstruction. In MRI reconstruction, Mamba-based designs are commonly combined with data consistency constraints so that the network can exploit both learned feature representations and the acquired k-space measurements [17]. Although these methods have shown strong potential in global context modeling and physics-guided reconstruction, most existing designs mainly focus on the main reconstruction stages. The output stage is less explicitly explored, especially for local error compensation, edge-detail preservation, and residual high-frequency artifact suppression. Motivated by this observation, AGGR-Mamba keeps the Mamba backbone as the main reconstruction component and introduces lightweight output-stage refinement modules to improve local structure recovery and refinement stability. III. M ETHODOLOGY 

A. Mamba Backbone 

AGGR-Mamba adopts a Mamba-based reconstruction net-work as the backbone [17]. The backbone is organized as a six-stage unrolled reconstruction network. Each reconstruction stage consists of one VSSLayer followed by one Data Con-sistency (DC) module, where each VSSLayer contains two VSSBlocks. The VSSBlocks are used to model long-range dependencies and global contextual information, while the DC modules enforce consistency between the intermediate recon-struction and the acquired k-space measurements. Therefore, the backbone contains 12 VSSBlocks and six stage-wise DC modules in total. After the six stages, an additional final DC module is applied for data consistency enforcement. Based on the backbone output, AGGR-Mamba further applies the Attention Refine Head and the GDFN-style gated residual refinement branch to compensate for remaining local errors at the output stage. For simplicity, each “VSSM Block” illustrated in Fig. 2 represents a VSSLayer containing two VSSBlocks. B. Implementation Details 

The model used in the main experiments is AGGR-Mamba. Built on the Mamba backbone, AGGR-Mamba further incor-porates an Attention Refine Head and a GDFN-style gated residual refinement branch at the output stage. In the final optimization stage, the trained backbone is frozen and only the Attention Refine Head and GDFN-style branch are updated. These modules refine the backbone reconstruction through local feature recalibration and gated residual compensation. The comparison methods include UNet [10], E2E-VarNet [21], SwinUNET [27], SwinMR [28], and Recon-Former [22]. Among them, UNet serves as a representative CNN-based reconstruction baseline, E2E-VarNet represents physics-guided unrolled reconstruction methods, SwinUNET and SwinMR are included as Transformer-based reconstruction methods, and ReconFormer represents recurrent Transformer-based MRI reconstruction. By covering CNNs, unrolled net-works, Transformers, and recurrent Transformer models, these comparisons provide a more comprehensive evaluation of AGGR-Mamba on undersampled brain MRI reconstruction using the IXI dataset.

To make the output-stage refinement focus more on struc-turally informative regions, the Attention Refine Head in-troduces channel attention and spatial attention at its end. Channel attention generates channel-wise weights using global average pooling and max pooling, while spatial attention produces spatial weights from the average and maximum responses along the channel dimension. This process can be written as 

Fa = SA (CA ( Fr )) , (12) where CA( ·) and SA( ·) denote channel attention and spatial attention, respectively. Finally, the Attention Refine Head predicts a two-channel residual term, which is added to the backbone output with a learnable scaling factor sa:

ra = Pout (Fa), (13)

In the implementation, the final output convolution is initialized with zeros, so that the module behaves approximately as an identity mapping at the beginning of optimization. This reduces the initial distur-bance introduced by output-stage refinement to the backbone reconstruction. 

C. GDFN-style Gated Residual Refinement Branch 

The Attention Refine Head provides an initial recalibration of the output features in both channel and spatial dimensions. On this basis, a GDFN-style gated residual refinement branch is introduced to further screen the candidate residual infor-mation. Through gated feature selection, the branch preserves useful detail residuals while filtering redundant or unstable candidate residual responses, thereby reducing their interfer-ence with the final reconstruction. More specifically, the input xa is first projected by a convo-lution layer to obtain intermediate features. A 1×1 convolution is then used to expand the channel dimension, followed by a depth-wise convolution for local context encoding: 

F = DWConv ( Pe (GELU( Pg (xa)))) , (15) where Pg denotes the input projection, Pe denotes the feature expansion operation, and DWConv denotes the depth-wise convolution. The expanded features are then split into two branches along the channel dimension: 

[F1, F2] = Split( F). (16) The gated features are obtained by element-wise multiplica-tion: 

Fg = GELU( F1) ⊙ F2. (17) After that, Fg is mapped back to a two-channel residual through the feature projection and output projection layers:

Table I reports the quantitative comparison of different methods on the IXI-T1 and IXI-T2 modalities under the 

R = 4 and R = 8 undersampling settings. Overall, AGGR-Mamba achieves competitive results across all four testing settings, with particularly stable performance on the T2-weighted reconstruction tasks. For IXI-T2 R = 4 , AGGR-Mamba reaches 46.99 dB in PSNR, higher than the 46.71 dB achieved by ReconFormer, and ranks first among all methods in terms of PSNR. Its SSIM(%) is 99.45, which is slightly below ReconFormer’s 99.53. For IXI-T2 R = 8 ,AGGR-Mamba again reports the best PSNR, with 38.48 dB versus 38.34 dB for ReconFormer. This result suggests that, for the T2 modality, output-stage attention recalibration and gated residual compensation can effectively refine the initial reconstruction produced by the Mamba backbone. AGGR-Mamba also maintains a strong performance on the IXI-T1 tasks. Under the IXI-T1 R = 4 setting, it obtains a PSNR of 41.94 dB and an SSIM(%) of 99.11, which are close to ReconFormer’s 42.36 dB and 99.24. In the IXI-T1 R = 8 

setting, E2E-VarNet achieves the best PSNR, while Recon-Former gives the best SSIM. AGGR-Mamba obtains a PSNR of 35.39 dB, outperforming ReconFormer at 33.87 dB and ranking second among all compared methods. These results suggest that AGGR-Mamba remains stable across different modalities and undersampling rates. Its advantage is more evident in PSNR improvement and residual error correction, although the structural similarity metric in some T1 settings still leaves room for further improvement. From the perspective of model types, UNet and SwinUNET generally obtain lower quantitative scores, which suggests that pure CNN reconstruction and directly transferred U-shaped Transformer designs remain limited on the current IXI task. SwinMR improves over UNet and SwinUNET in most cases, but its performance is still behind ReconFormer and AGGR-Mamba. E2E-VarNet performs well on IXI-T1 R = 8 ,reflecting the benefit of physics-guided unrolled networks in enforcing data consistency under certain high-acceleration settings. By comparison, AGGR-Mamba first produces a sta-ble reconstruction through the Mamba backbone and then improves PSNR with output-stage attention recalibration and gated residual compensation. Its SSIM remains second-best or close to the best result in several settings, suggesting that the proposed lightweight refinement design provides useful compensation for local reconstruction errors. 

D. Analysis of Experimental Results 

Figure 3 shows the reconstructed images, zoomed-in re-gions, and error maps of different methods under the IXI-T2 R = 4 setting. As can be seen, the error maps of UNet, SwinUNET, and SwinMR still contain noticeable bright regions, indicating that residual errors remain around local textures and edges. E2E-VarNet and ReconFormer produce lower errors, and their reconstructed images are closer to the target image in terms of overall structure. By contrast, the error map of AGGR-Mamba is generally darker, and the brain tissue boundaries and texture patterns in the zoomed-in regions are also clearer. Its advantage is mainly reflected in reduced error-map intensity and more stable local structures. This suggests that the output-stage refinement modules further correct the initial reconstruction produced by the Mamba backbone, rather than improving the metrics by substantially changing the global image contrast. 

E. Ablation Study Analysis

These differences describe the re-ported checkpoints and do not establish statistical significance. V. D ISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION 

AGGR-Mamba refines the output of a trained Mamba reconstruction backbone through channel-spatial attention and GDFN-style gated residual filtering. Final-stage optimization updates only the two refinement modules while keeping the backbone fixed. On the evaluated IXI-T2 settings, the method achieves PSNR values of 46.99 dB at R = 4 and 38.48 dB at 

R = 8 . The IXI-T2 R = 4 ablation identifies Attention Refine as the main source of improvement, with smaller additional gains from GDFN and joint fine-tuning. The findings are limited to the evaluated IXI single-coil brain MRI settings. Performance is more favorable on T2 images, and the method does not achieve the highest SSIM in every setting. The small differences between fine-tuning strategies require further validation before drawing conclu-sions about stability.
