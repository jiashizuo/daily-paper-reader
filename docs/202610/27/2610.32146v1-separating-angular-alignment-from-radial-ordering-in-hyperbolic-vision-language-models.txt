ABSTRACT
Hyperbolic vision–language models are well suited to modeling the hierarchi-
cal structure of multimodal data, because hyperbolic volume grows exponentially
with radius, enabling low-distortion representations of tree-like taxonomies from
general to specific concepts. However, existing pretraining objectives for hyper-
bolic vision–language models entangle semantic alignment with entailment mod-
eling, forcing radial coordinates to simultaneously encode semantic matching and
hierarchical structure, leading to conflicting geometric constraints. In this pa-
per, we introduce AURORA, a hyperbolic vision–language framework that decou-
ples the objectives through angular alignment and a novel Radial–Angular Order
Cone (RAOC) for entailment modeling. Angular alignment captures semantic
similarity while preserving the radial structure for representing hierarchies. Un-
like conventional entailment cones, RAOC determines angular allowance from
relative parent–child radial gaps. It is incorporated into hierarchical entailment
contrastive training to capture partial-order relations. We further introduce a norm
regularization term that prevents undesirable radial degeneration for stable train-
ing. We train models on millions of image–text pairs and evaluate them across
zero-shot classification, image–text retrieval, hierarchical reasoning, and compo-
sitional understanding. Our model consistently outperforms strong Euclidean and
hyperbolic vision–language baselines, demonstrating the effectiveness of decou-
pling semantic alignment from hierarchical entailment modeling.

INTRODUCTION
Hyperbolic vision–language models, such as MERU (Desai et al., 2023) and HyCoCLIP (Pal et al.,
2025), embed images and text in hyperbolic spaces (negatively curved Riemannian manifolds) (Rat-
cliffe, 1994) to represent hierarchical relations among visual and linguistic concepts. In the Lorentz
model (Cannon et al., 1997), a model of hyperbolic space, distance from the origin serves as a
proxy for semantic specificity, with more general concepts represented at smaller radii (Nickel &
Kiela, 2017). Under the hyperbolic entailment convention, parent–child relations form a partial
order (Vendrov et al., 2016): abstract parents entail more specific children, whose embeddings lie
within their parents’ entailment cones (Ganea et al., 2018). For example, the text “a dog” entails
the more specific description “a dog running on grass”, and an image depicting “a dog running on
grass” is compatible with both descriptions. Modeling this partial order allows matching visual and
textual concepts to occupy different levels of the hierarchy, reflecting their differences in specificity.
Hyperbolic volume grows exponentially with radius (Krioukov et al., 2010), accommodating the
increasing number of child nodes with depth. Hyperbolic space also enables low-distortion tree em-
beddings (Sarkar, 2012; Sala et al., 2018), making it naturally suitable for representing hierarchies.
Learning the hierarchical structures of data requires compatible objectives for semantic alignment
and hierarchical entailment. However, existing hyperbolic pretraining objectives impose conflicting
constraints on radial information: semantic alignment favors similar radii for matched represen-
tations, while hierarchical entailment uses radial separation to distinguish parents from children.

Moreover, entailment supervision based on positive pairs encourages specified parent–child rela-
tions while leaving unpaired relations unpenalized. As a result, semantically unrelated representa-
tions may still form spurious hierarchical relations. For example, in Figure 1, the text “apple” could
incorrectly entail an image depicting “a rocket successfully lifting off”.
We analyze the geometric compatibility of Lorentz-distance alignment and conventional entailment
cones to clarify the optimization conflict, identifying two limitations. Firstly, minimizing Lorentz
distance between a matched parent–child pair favors reducing the radial gap that models their dif-
ference in specificity. Conversely, an unmatched child can remain inside a spurious parent’s cone
even when moved arbitrarily far outward along its radial direction. These cases reveal a mismatch
between semantic discrimination and hierarchical entailment. Secondly, conventional cone aper-
tures (Le et al., 2019) are inversely coupled to the norm of the parent representation. As this norm
approaches the admissible lower limit, the cone becomes less restrictive for partial-order relations.
In this paper, we propose AURORA, a pretraining framework that separates AngUlar alignment from
Radial Ordering in hypeRbolic vision–lAnguage models. AURORA learns semantic alignment and
hierarchical entailment through distinct, complementary objectives. For semantic matching, we
perform angular alignment using cosine similarity between the spatial directions of Lorentz embed-
dings, allowing their radial positions to model specificity. For hierarchical entailment, we introduce
the Radial–Angular Order Cone, which controls angular allowance through the parent–child radial
gap, replacing the dependence on the parent’s norm in conventional cones. We prove that the result-
ing geometric entailment relation defines a partial order with nested descendant regions, ensuring
that a child’s descendants are also descendants of its parent. We further introduce hierarchical en-
tailment contrastive training, using negative pairs to suppress spurious entailment between unpaired
examples. Finally, we present a norm regularization term that prevents undesirable radial degenera-
tion, stabilizing the radial scale during training. Our contributions are summarized as follows:
• We introduce AURORA, a hyperbolic vision–language pretraining framework that decouples an-
gular semantic alignment from radial modeling of hierarchical entailment.
• We define the Radial–Angular Order Cone (RAOC), prove that its geometric entailment relation
defines a partial order, and develop hierarchical entailment contrastive (HEC) training on RAOC
to better reinforce hierarchical relations.
• We train hyperbolic vision–language models with AURORA and demonstrate consistent improve-
ments in zero-shot classification, retrieval, hierarchical reasoning, and compositional understand-
ing over competitive Euclidean and hyperbolic baselines.

The first term depends only on the radial gap, and the second depends on angular separation and

Conventional cones depend on more than pairwise distance. Conventional hyperbolic entailment
cones admit descendants within an angular region extending outward from the parent (Ganea et al.,
2018; Desai et al., 2023). Their membership is not determined by distance: two distinct embeddings
at the same radial depth can be arbitrarily close yet remain outside each other’s cones, whereas a
child on the parent’s outward radial ray remains inside its cone at arbitrarily large distances. Con-
sequently, bringing a matched pair closer does not ensure entailment, and pushing an unrelated pair
farther apart does not ensure its exclusion from the descendant region. With positive-only entail-
ment supervision, such erroneous relations receive no direct entailment penalty. Cone selectivity
also depends on the parent’s absolute depth: for a fixed cone-width setting, the half-aperture satis-
fies sin ω(p) ∝1/ sinh ρp. As the parent moves inward toward its admissible lower radius, ω(p)
approaches π/2, admitting the entire outward hemisphere of geodesic directions at the boundary.
Parent contraction can therefore weaken angular discrimination. The distance counterexamples and
aperture analysis are provided in Appendix B.1.

AURORA
4.1
FORMULATION
Following the training setup of HyCoCLIP (Pal et al., 2025), we use image–caption pairs (I, T) with
object-level annotations. A bounding box in I specifies an object crop Ib, and the corresponding
phrase T b describes its content. Depicted in Figure 2, AURORA uses a visual encoder fv and a
text encoder ft, followed by projections Pv and Pt, respectively. Images and crops share the visual
branch, while captions and phrases share the text branch. For an input X ∈{I, T, Ib, T b}, let f and
P denote the corresponding encoder and projector. We obtain the projected feature zX = P(f(X))
and its hyperbolic representation xX = expc
O(0, zX).
Hierarchy supervision uses the relations types R = {T →I, T b →Ib, Ib →I, T b →T}, with
arrows pointing from general to specific. Text is treated as more general than its visual counterpart,
which contains additional instance details. Likewise, crops and phrases are treated as more general
than full images and captions, which add scene context. We align matching visual and textual
representations through root-angle alignment (Sec. 4.2) and learn the hierarchical relations in R
through RAOC and HEC training (Secs. 4.3–4.4). Norm regularization controls their radial scale.

5
EXPERIMENTS
5.1
TRAINING DETAILS
We pretrain AURORA on GRIT (Peng et al., 2024), comprising 10.3M valid image–caption pairs
and 17.8M grounded box–phrase annotations. We mainly report results using ViT-S/16, with com-
plete comparisons for both ViT-S/16 and ViT-B/16 provided in Appendix G. Training schedules,
hyperparameters, and implementation details are given in Appendix F. We compare AURORA with
CLIP (Radford et al., 2021), SigLIP (Zhai et al., 2023), MERU (Desai et al., 2023), ATMG (Ra-
masinghe et al., 2024), HyCoCLIP (Pal et al., 2025), PHyCLIP (Yoshikawa & Matsubara, 2026),
and ARGENT (Huynh et al., 2026). For CLIP, SigLIP, and MERU, we include variants pretrained
with and without box–phrase pairs to assess the contribution of additional grounded supervision.
All comparisons are conducted under the same task-specific evaluation protocols. AURORA uses
root-angle similarity s∠for all similarity-based evaluations.

RELATED WORK
Vision and language representation learning. CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) and ALIGN (Jia et al.,
2021) learn transferable visual and textual representations through contrastive learning on image
and text pairs. SigLIP (Zhai et al., 2023) replaces softmax normalization with independent sigmoid
losses over pairs. RegionCLIP (Zhong et al., 2022) extends alignment to image regions and textual
concepts. AURORA retains separate image and text encoders and examines how semantic alignment
interacts with hierarchical supervision.
Hyperbolic vision and language representations. Hyperbolic embeddings capture hierarchical
structure in language and vision (Nickel & Kiela, 2017; Khrulkov et al., 2020). MERU combines
Lorentz representations with entailment constraints (Desai et al., 2023). HyCoCLIP incorporates
object regions and grounded phrases through compositional contrastive and entailment learning (Pal
et al., 2025). PHyCLIP uses an ℓ1 product of hyperbolic factors to model hierarchy and compo-
sition (Yoshikawa & Matsubara, 2026). Wu et al. (Wu et al., 2026) align visual and textual trees
across hyperbolic manifolds with different curvatures. ATMG addresses the tension between dis-
tance alignment and entailment through an exterior angle contrastive objective (Ramasinghe et al.,
2024). AURORA separates root angle alignment from radial ordering to preserve differences in
specificity.
Hierarchical representations and entailment regions. Hierarchical embeddings encode asymmet-
ric relations through coordinatewise partial orders (Vendrov et al., 2016). Hyperbolic entailment
cones define nested descendant regions (Ganea et al., 2018) and support concept hierarchy induction
from text (Le et al., 2019). Shadow Cones (Yu et al., 2024) generalizes entailment regions through
umbral and penumbral constructions based on geometric occlusion. ARGENT (Huynh et al., 2026)
combines adaptive entailment supervision with norm regularization to stabilize hierarchical repre-
sentations. AURORA defines descendant membership through relative radial separation and root
angle difference, with contrastive supervision over matched and unmatched pairs.

CONCLUSION
We introduced AURORA, a hyperbolic vision–language pretraining framework that separates an-
gular semantic alignment from radial hierarchical ordering. Our analysis shows that distance min-
imization can conflict with hierarchical separation, while semantic discrimination alone does not
ensure valid entailment. AURORA combines angular alignment with the Radial–Angular Order
Cone, which defines a partial order through radial gaps and angular differences. Hierarchical entail-
ment contrastive training reinforces annotated relations and suppresses spurious entailment, while
norm regularization stabilizes training. Experiments demonstrate improvements over competitive
Euclidean and hyperbolic baselines across classification, retrieval, hierarchical classification, and

across image–text examples and explore how to learn from them with fewer explicit annotations.

RELATION TO RECENT HYPERBOLIC VISION–LANGUAGE OBJECTIVES
Exterior-angle alignment. ATMG (Ramasinghe et al., 2024) analyzes the tension between
geodesic-proximity alignment and hierarchical organization and introduces an exterior-angle con-
trastive objective. Its score reduces the dependence of semantic matching on absolute radial position.
AURORA uses the common-origin root angle, whose Lorentz and Poincaré realizations are given in
Eq. (20); RAOC then carries the radial structure required for hierarchical ordering.
Parent-dependent entailment cones. MERU and HyCoCLIP employ EC descendant regions
whose aperture varies with the parent norm, while PHyCLIP applies hierarchy constraints in a factor-
ized hyperbolic representation (Desai et al., 2023; Pal et al., 2025; Yoshikawa & Matsubara, 2026).
Section B.1 shows how the EC aperture broadens as the parent approaches its admissible inner ra-
dius. ARGENT (Huynh et al., 2026) addresses this training regime with adaptive exterior-angle
supervision and radial stabilization, and a later operating-point audit reports saturation or weak
activation of conventional cone/traversal mechanisms in several released hyperbolic VLM check-
points (Kim et al., 2026c). RAOC parameterizes descendant selectivity directly through the relative
radial gap and root angle; the resulting relation admits the transitivity and descendant-set nesting
guarantees in Theorem 1.
Uncertainty-guided compositional alignment. UNCHA is a concurrent 2026 method that models
part-to-whole semantic representativeness through a norm-derived uncertainty signal (Kim et al.,
2026a).
The uncertainty weights its Lorentz-distance contrastive alignment and calibrates an
20


Preprint
entropy-regularized entailment term. Its entailment branch is formulated around observed part–
whole relations, whereas AURORA assigns entailment labels to both matched and unmatched
parent–candidate pairs. The two methods therefore organize compositional supervision differently:
UNCHA modulates distance-based alignment according to part representativeness, and AURORA
separates semantic matching into root-angle alignment while learning radial order through con-
trastive RAOC supervision.
During our reproduction, we found that the public uncha/data/webdataset_mapper.py
contains the same evaluation-dataset implementation as uncha/data/evaluation.py, leav-
ing the training WebDataset mapper implementation absent from the released code path (Kim et al.,
2026b). We reconstructed the mapper from the data pipeline described in the paper and the sur-
rounding training interfaces. Since the resulting implementation did not consistently reproduce the
reported trends across repeated runs, we exclude reproduced UNCHA numbers from the controlled
comparison tables to avoid comparisons based on an implementation whose fidelity could not be
fully verified.

EVALUATION PROTOCOLS
H.1
ZERO-SHOT IMAGE CLASSIFICATION
We evaluate the same 16 classification datasets for every checkpoint: ImageNet (Deng et al.,
2009), CIFAR-10 (Krizhevsky, 2009), CIFAR-100 (Krizhevsky, 2009), SUN397 (Xiao et al., 2010),
Caltech-101 (Fei-Fei et al., 2004), STL-10 (Coates et al., 2011), Food-101 (Bossard et al., 2014),
CUB-200-2011 (Wah et al., 2011), Stanford Cars (Krause et al., 2013), FGVC Aircraft (Maji et al.,
2013), Oxford-IIIT Pets (Parkhi et al., 2012), Oxford Flowers-102 (Nilsback & Zisserman, 2008),
DTD (Cimpoi et al., 2014), EuroSAT (Helber et al., 2019), RESISC45 (Cheng et al., 2017), and
Country211 (Radford et al., 2021). For a given dataset, all models use the same class vocabulary
and prompt templates. AURORA scores image embeddings against text prototypes using s∠; each
baseline is evaluated with the similarity function native to that model. We report top-1 accuracy.
H.2
IMAGE–TEXT RETRIEVAL
Bidirectional retrieval is evaluated on the COCO validation set (Lin et al., 2014) and the Flickr30K
test set (Young et al., 2014). In text-to-image retrieval, each caption queries the image collection;
in image-to-text retrieval, each image queries the caption collection. We report Recall@5 and Re-
call@10 in both directions, matching the columns in the main and appendix tables. AURORA ranks
candidates by root-angle similarity, while each baseline retains its native released retrieval score.

H.5
SUGARCREPE
SugarCrepe (Hsieh et al., 2023) evaluates image-to-text compositional discrimination using flu-

ADDITIONAL HIERARCHY VISUALIZATIONS
We provide additional visualizations of AURORA ViT-S with and without RAOC to examine se-
mantic alignment and hierarchical organization together. Each scatter plot shows the root angle θ to
a fixed query and the radial gap g between modalities. Smaller root angles indicate stronger seman-
tic alignment. Dashed lines connect concepts or image regions according to the displayed hierarchy,
allowing their radial order to be inspected alongside their similarity to the query. Orange squares
denote related alternatives, and green crosses denote unrelated controls. The original axis ranges are
retained for each model.
I.1
IMAGE TO TEXT
Figures 6 and 7 compare ten image queries. Each row shows the same example with RAOC on the
left and without RAOC on the right. Blue circles trace the semantic path from a broad category to
the query class. With RAOC, these paths consistently move toward smaller radial gaps and smaller
root angles as the concepts become more specific. Without RAOC, the query class can still have
a small root angle, while the radial sequence reverses in examples such as electric guitar, leopard,
monarch, and zebra. The piano, laptop, and desk examples retain an ordered path in both variants.
Together, these comparisons support the role of RAOC in making hierarchical order more consistent
while preserving semantic alignment.
I.2
TEXT TO IMAGE
Figures 8–12 compare five text queries: shoes, eggplant, earphone, chair, and bread. Each figure
places the model with RAOC above the model without RAOC. The image region hierarchy on the
right provides the same reference structure for both models, and the colored dashed links identify its
two branches in the scatter plot. With RAOC, the branch containing the queried object follows an
increasing radial gap and a decreasing root angle from the full image through the intermediate region
to the object crop. Without RAOC, angular alignment often remains, but the radial progression can
reverse or the intermediate region can be less aligned than the full image. These examples provide
qualitative evidence that RAOC couples semantic alignment with the radial organization associated
with hierarchical partial order.
