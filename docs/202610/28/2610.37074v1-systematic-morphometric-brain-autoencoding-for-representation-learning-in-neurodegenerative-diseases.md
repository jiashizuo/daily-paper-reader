## 摘要
利用结构T1加权MRI对神经退行性疾病进行早期识别对于及时临床干预至关重要，但监督式深度学习模型常因标注有限和扫描仪引起的分布偏移而难以泛化。自监督学习提供了一种有前景的替代方案；然而，现有方法大多改编自自然图像范式，依赖对比目标或采用体素级损失的通用掩码重建，可能无法充分捕捉与神经退行性变相关的分布式形态学模式。我们提出SYMBA（SYstematic Morphometric Brain Autoencoding，系统性形态学脑自编码），一种专为结构脑MRI设计的自监督预训练框架。SYMBA整合了解剖学约束掩码、多尺度结构相似性（MS-SSIM）重建目标，以及从局部解剖细节逐步过渡到全局脑形态的渐进式课程。这些组件将神经解剖结构显式纳入自监督目标，而非依赖通用图像先验。SYMBA在来自九个异质队列的6,953例T1加权MRI扫描上预训练，并在两个独立的未见数据集上使用冻结线性探测进行评估，从而能够严格评估分布外泛化能力。在多个临床任务中，SYMBA始终优于近期神经影像基础模型，在阿尔茨海默病分类中达到0.930 AUROC，在轻度认知障碍转化预测中达到0.772，并在淀粉样蛋白状态预测中表现良好。在低标签场景下，SYMBA表现出高样本效率，仅使用六名有标签受试者即可在AD/CN分类中达到约0.90 AUROC。

## Abstract
Early identification of neurodegenerative disorders using structural T1-weighted MRI is essential for timely clinical intervention, yet supervised deep learning models often struggle to generalize due to limited annotations and scanner-induced distribution shift. Self-supervised learning offers a promising alternative; however, existing approaches are largely adapted from natural image paradigms, relying on contrastive objectives or generic masked reconstruction with voxel-wise losses, which may not fully capture the distributed morphometric patterns associated with neurodegeneration.   We introduce SYMBA (SYstematic Morphometric Brain Autoencoding), a self-supervised pretraining framework specifically designed for structural brain MRI. SYMBA integrates anatomically constrained masking, a multi-scale structural similarity (MS-SSIM) reconstruction objective, and a progressive curriculum that transitions from local anatomical detail to global brain morphology. These components explicitly incorporate neuroanatomical structure into the self-supervised objective rather than relying on generic image priors.   Pretrained on 6,953 T1-weighted MRI scans from nine heterogeneous cohorts, SYMBA is evaluated using frozen linear probing on two independent unseen datasets, enabling a strict assessment of out-of-distribution generalization. Across multiple clinical tasks, SYMBA consistently outperforms recent neuroimaging foundation models, achieving 0.930 AUROC for Alzheimer's disease classification, 0.772 for mild cognitive impairment conversion prediction, and strong performance in amyloid status prediction. In low-label regimes, SYMBA demonstrates high sample efficiency, reaching approximately 0.90 AUROC for AD/CN classification using only six labeled subjects.
