## 摘要
三维解剖变化的时空预测是建模阿尔茨海默病（AD）进展的一项关键生成式视觉挑战。虽然基于单模态数据的脑部MRI预测已取得进展，但其他关键互补的多模态生物标志物仍未得到充分利用。在本工作中，我们提出多模态条件潜在扩散模型（MC-LDM），这是一种用于患者特异性脑部MRI合成的新框架。MC-LDM在训练期间利用多模态生物标志物——MRI、tau-PET、临床痴呆评定量表（CDR）和APOE4——但在推理时仅依赖基线MRI、APOE4和患者元数据，这反映了现实临床可用性。我们的贡献有三方面：（1）通过低秩适应（LoRA）微调的潜在扩散模型（LDM），以实现数据高效学习；（2）多模态条件化，整合分子和临床信号以建模异质性疾病的轨迹；（3）辅助预测器，从MRI特征和患者协变量估计未观测的多模态生物标志物，以对扩散模型进行条件化，从而在推理时无需这些评估。MC-LDM在阿尔茨海默病神经影像学倡议（ADNI）中852名受试者的1,355次访视上训练，在包含664次访视的独立测试集上，相较第二佳基线将平均区域体积MAE降低18%。据我们所知，MC-LDM是首个利用多模态先验进行MRI预测的扩散框架。这一概念性进展使得能够生成患者特异性轨迹，捕获仅从结构解剖无法观察到的AD异质性方面，弥合了丰富的多模态监督与常规临床部署严格约束之间的差距。代码见：https://github.com/antonioscardace/MC-LDM。

## Abstract
Spatiotemporal forecasting of 3D anatomical changes is a critical generative vision challenge for modeling Alzheimer's Disease (AD) progression. While brain MRI forecasting from single-modality data has advanced, other key complementary multimodal biomarkers remain underutilized. In this work, we propose the Multimodal-Conditioned Latent Diffusion Model (MC-LDM), a novel framework for patient-specific brain MRI synthesis. MC-LDM leverages multimodal biomarkers---MRI, tau-PET, Clinical Dementia Rating (CDR), and APOE4---during training, but relies only on baseline MRI, APOE4, and patient metadata at inference, reflecting realistic clinical availability. Our contributions are threefold: (1) a Latent Diffusion Model (LDM) fine-tuned via Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) for data-efficient learning; (2) a multimodal conditioning that integrates molecular and clinical signals to model heterogeneous disease trajectories; and (3) auxiliary predictors estimating unobserved multimodal biomarkers from MRI features and patient covariates to condition the diffusion model, removing the need for these assessments at inference. Trained on 1,355 visits from 852 subjects in the Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI), MC-LDM reduces average regional volumetric MAE by 18% over the second-best baseline on an independent test set of 664 visits. To our knowledge, MC-LDM represents the first diffusion-based framework to leverage multimodal priors for MRI forecasting. This conceptual advancement enables patient-specific trajectory generation that captures aspects of AD heterogeneity not observable from structural anatomy alone, bridging the gap between rich multimodal supervision and the strict constraints of routine clinical deployment.
