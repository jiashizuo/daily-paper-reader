# Beyond Phenotypic Similarity: Relation Refinement and Selective Population Graph Learning for Multimodal Brain Disorder Diagnosis 

Yinyu Lan 

School of Medicine and Health, Harbin Institute of Technology Harbin 150001, China lanyinyu19@mails.ucas.ac.cn 

Abstract —Population graph learning supports brain disorder diagnosis by sharing information across subjects. Its effectiveness, however, depends on whether the relationships guiding this exchange are relevant to diagnosis. Similarity in demographic or acquisition attributes does not necessarily indicate diagnostic agreement, and heterogeneous subjects may require different representation updates within the same population graph. We propose PopDRP, a framework that combines diagnosis-guided relation refinement with hierarchical selective state-space learn-ing. Using only training labels, Contrastive Affinity Refine-ment calibrates attribute-based affinities according to diagnostic agreement. Neural Graph Rectification then refines these rela-tionships using fused functional-connectivity and non-imaging representations, guided by graph reconstruction and dual-view alignment objectives. On the resulting graph, a Selective Pyramid State-Space backbone integrates input-dependent state updates, graph diffusion, and hierarchical pooling and unpooling to learn population representations at multiple resolutions. Together, these components connect the refinement of inter-subject relationships with selective representation learning over the population graph. Experiments on ABIDE and ADHD-200 under stratified ten-fold cross-validation show improved accuracy and AUC over the compared baselines. Ablation studies further support the contributions of relation refinement and the selective hierarchical backbone to classification performance. 

Index Terms —Population graph learning, brain disorder diag-nosis, multimodal learning, graph structure refinement, selective state-space models. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Population graph learning provides a way to incorporate inter-subject information into brain disorder diagnosis [1], [2]. In this formulation, each node represents a subject, node features describe individual measurements such as resting-state functional connectivity, and edges determine how information is shared across the cohort. Non-imaging attributes, includ-ing age, sex, and acquisition site, provide additional cues for constructing these relationships [2], [3]. The value of a population graph therefore depends not only on the quality of individual features, but also on whether the relationships connecting subjects support the classification task. A central modeling difficulty is that attribute similarity and diagnostic relevance are not interchangeable . Two sub-jects may share demographic characteristics or an acquisition site while having different diagnoses. Existing research has already moved beyond fixed graph construction: EV-GCN learns adaptive inter-subject associations [3], while MM-GTUNets incorpo-rates label-informed affinity modeling and hierarchical graph encoding [4]. Building on these advances, we investigate how diagnosis-guided affinities can be further refined using multimodal representations and incorporated into a selective hierarchical population encoder. Relation refinement and population representation learning are closely connected. Edge weights determine which subjects influence one another, while the encoder determines how the resulting information is transformed. A refined graph provides a basis for information exchange, but heterogeneous subject features still motivate input-dependent representation updates. Selective state-space models offer a mechanism for condition-ing state transformations on the input, and their adaptation to graph learning has motivated investigation into graph-aware information processing [5]–[7]. Hierarchical graph processing introduces a further consideration: representations should in-tegrate information at different population resolutions while retaining features needed for individual predictions [4], [8]. These considerations motivate a framework that combines feature-conditioned relation refinement with selective state updates throughout a graph hierarchy. We present PopDRP to implement this design through three connected stages. First, Contrastive Affinity Refinement arXiv:2610.30902v1 [cs.AI] 22 Oct 2026 (CAR) uses diagnostic agreement among training subjects to calibrate non-imaging affinities, producing a diagnosis-guided population prior. Second, Neural Graph Rectification (NGR) combines this prior with fused imaging and non-imaging features to learn refined edge weights. Graph reconstruction and dual-view alignment constrain this process. Third, the Selective Pyramid State-Space (SPSS) backbone processes the refined graph through input-dependent state updates and topology-aware diffusion within a pooling and unpooling hierarchy. Skip connections retain subject-level information as graph resolution changes. The resulting architecture links the refinement of population relationships to selective representa-tion learning across the hierarchy. The contributions of this work are threefold:  

> •

We develop a population graph refinement pipeline that combines training-label-informed affinities with feature-conditioned edge learning. This allows multimodal sub-ject representations to refine the relationships used for downstream classification.  

> •

We introduce a Selective Pyramid State-Space backbone that integrates input-dependent state updates and graph diffusion within a hierarchical encoder–decoder. It learns population representations at multiple resolutions while retaining information for subject-level prediction.  

> •

We evaluate PopDRP on ABIDE and ADHD-200 using stratified ten-fold cross-validation. Comparisons with es-tablished baselines and component ablations assess the effectiveness of the framework and the contributions of its principal components. II. R ELATED WORK 

A. Population Graph-Based Brain Disease Diagnosis 

Parisot et al. pioneered population graph learning for brain disease diagnosis via handcrafted non-imaging similarity and GCN-based classification [1], [2]. Follow-up works optimized this framework via multi-scale graph convolution [9], ROI-aware graph construction [10], multimodal fusion, graph trans-former and adversarial contrastive learning [3], [4], [11], [12]. Even so, most methods build population graphs with fixed handcrafted similarity metrics on raw non-imaging data, suffering severely from missing data, site effects and label noise. 

B. State-Space Models and Graph Mamba 

SSMs outperform attention models for long-range sequence modeling with linear computational complexity. Classic S4 and Mamba capture long-range dependencies via continuous latent states and input-selective gating [5], [13]. Extended graph Mamba methods serialize graph nodes into sequences to fit Mamba blocks for graph learning [6], [7], yet they only support single-scale modeling without hierarchical graph pooling. To tackle this issue, our SPSS backbone embeds selective SSM units into a U-Net pyramid structure and adopts graph diffusion for topology-aware state propagation, support-ing joint multi-scale and long-range representation learning for population graphs. 

C. Graph Denoising and Structure Learning 

Graph structure learning jointly optimizes graph topology and node embeddings [14]. Existing graph rectification meth-ods refine graph edges relying on node features or inherent topology [15], [16], which are tailored for homogeneous graphs and fail to introduce diagnostic label supervision. Besides, population graphs face unique noise from defective non-imaging attributes and site confounding factors. Our NGR module addresses this gap by modeling edge feature interac-tions and leveraging clinical label priors from CAR-optimized graphs under a minimum-sufficient-representation objective. III. ADHD-200 Dataset: The ADHD-200 consortium dataset [18] provides rs-fMRI from 582 subjects (364 healthy controls, 218 individuals with ADHD) across multiple sites. Stratified 10-fold cross-validation is employed, with an additional 10% of training data held out as a validation set for each fold. 

B. Evaluation Metrics and Baselines 

We evaluate performance using five metrics: classification accuracy (ACC), sensitivity (SEN), specificity (SPE), area under the ROC curve (AUC), and F1-score. We compare PopDRP against three categories of methods: We compare PopDRP against three categories of base-lines. Traditional machine learning methods: SVM with RBF kernel and MLP applied to the RFE-selected connectivity features. Brain-graph-based methods: Brain-GNN [10], DGCN [19], AL-NEGAT [20], and A-GCL [12], which construct individual-level brain graphs and apply GNNs for classifica-tion. Population-graph-based methods: Pop-GCN [2], GATE [21], EV-GCN [3], MMGL [11], and MM-GTUNets [4], which model inter-subject relationships as population graphs. To ensure consistency, the same data splits and evaluation protocol are adopted. 

C. Implementation Details 

The VAE used for non-imaging feature encoding includes a two-layer MLP encoder and a two-layer MLP decoder. We pre-train this VAE for a maximum of 3000 epochs and adopt early stopping with a patience value of 100. The CAR module applies learnable affine transformations αs, β s, γ s to each attribute across three contrastive decomposition channels for positive, negative and cross-split signals, while the learn-able edge temperature τ is initialized to 1.0. Neural Graph Rectification (NGR) uses a shared two-layer node encoder to produce dual-view representations, along with a two-layer edge decoder. GELU is used as the activation function for both modules. The dual-view learning objective combines MSE loss for reconstruction and cosine similarity for alignment, and it requires no extra hyperparameters. D. Performance Comparison 

As shown in Table II, traditional machine learning methods including SVM and MLP achieve relatively weak overall performance across all metrics. These methods lack the ability to model complex pairwise relationships between subjects, which limits their effectiveness for brain disease diagnosis tasks. Methods built upon brain graphs deliver better results than classic machine learning approaches, yet they still cannot fully exploit cross-subject information for accurate prediction. Recent population-graph methods generally outperform ear-lier solutions, as they explicitly model connections among different subjects and make full use of population-level data. Among these competitors, MM-GTUNets obtains competitive results and ranks second on most evaluation metrics. Even so, our PopDRP achieves the best performance in most cases on both datasets. It yields higher accuracy, sensitivity and AUC values than all compared methods, and maintains a favorable specificity level. The consistent improvements demonstrate that the designed graph refinement and multi-scale representa-tion learning modules effectively capture reliable population-level patterns, leading to more robust and accurate diagnosis. 

E. Ablation Study 

We conduct ablation experiments on the ADHD-200 and ABIDE datasets to quantify the contribution of each key component in our PopDRP framework. The detailed results are presented in Table III. We first remove the NGR module and keep all other components unchanged. The model achieves lower accuracy and AUC on both datasets, which verifies that neural graph rec-tification effectively eliminates spurious edges and improves the quality of learned graph structures. When the CAR module is replaced by simple averaging over non-imaging similarities of individual attributes, overall performance drops noticeably. This outcome indicates that contrastive affinity refinement can produce more reliable graph priors compared with naive aggregation strategies. To examine the effect of the SPSS backbone, we substitute it with a standard graph transformer. The resulting performance experiences a substantial decline, demonstrating that the pro-posed selective pyramid state-space design is essential for capturing multi-scale topological features and long-range node dependencies. F. Hyperparameter Sensitivity 

We conducted a systematic hyperparameter sensitivity anal-ysis on four key parameters: SPSS depth L, state dimension 

dstate , NGR mask rate, and hidden dimension. Each parameter was swept independently while holding all others at their default values, using 10-fold cross-validation on both datasets. Figure 4 presents the results for ADHD-200 and ABIDE separately. The analysis reveals dataset-specific optimal configurations. On ADHD-200, accuracy increases monotonically with SPSS depth, from 84.2% at L = 1 to 85.8% at L = 4 , suggesting that deeper state-space pyramids benefit datasets with lower site heterogeneity. The state dimension dstate = 64 provides the best accuracy-efficiency trade-off (85.1%), while smaller hidden dimensions (64) outperform larger ones (84.3% vs. 79.6% at 512), indicating that ADHD’s relatively homoge-neous acquisition does not require excessive model capacity. G. Visualization and Interpretability 

We provide four complementary visualizations to assess whether PopDRP learns clinically meaningful population structures and to validate the RFE-selected features against established neuroscience findings. 

PopDRP-selected brain connectome. Figure 5 shows the top-10 functional connectivity features identified by PopDRP on the glass brain for both datasets. On ADHD-200 (Figure 5a) the selected features concentrate on prefrontal, striatal and cin-gulate structures—the list contains IFG–amygdala, IFG–SMA and MFG–anterior-cingulate pairs, along with a cingulate– caudate connection, which is the classic fronto-striatal ADHD profile. These fronto-striatal and fronto-limbic pathways are well-established biomarkers of ADHD [22], reflecting the disorder’s core deficits in cognitive control, impulse regu-lation, and emotion processing. On ABIDE (Figure 5b) six of the ten features are cerebellar or vermian (two intra-vermis and four intra-cerebellar pairs) and three further involve orbitofrontal cortex, i.e. orbitofrontal–pallidum, orbitofrontal– precuneus and orbitofrontal–SMA. Cerebellar structural and functional abnormalities are among the most consistently replicated neuroimaging findings [23] in ASD, and the fronto-striatal (orbitofrontal-pallidal) circuit is centrally implicated in the repetitive behaviors and social cognition deficits character-istic of the disorder. The alignment between PopDRP-selected features and established neuroscience literature confirms that PopDRP’s feature selection captures diagnostically and bio-logically meaningful signal. 

t-SNE embedding visualization. Figure 3 presents t-SNE projections of the SPSS output embeddings. Each row corre-sponds to one dataset, with the left panel colored by diagnosis and the center panel colored by scanning site; the right panel provides a site-color legend. On both datasets, the diagnosis-colored panels show clear separation between healthy con-trols and patients, confirming that the SPSS backbone learns diagnostically discriminative representations. The site-colored panels reveal that subjects from different sites are intermingled within each diagnostic cluster rather than forming site-specific subclusters. 

H. Limitations and Future Work 

Several limitations motivate future research directions. First, while PopDRP currently models two diagnostic categories per dataset, extending to multi-class diagnosis and dimen-sional symptom severity prediction would increase clinical utility. Second, the population graph is constructed from a fixed set of non-imaging attributes; incorporating dynamic or longitudinal data could further personalize diagnosis. Third, all performance assessments in this paper are conducted via internal cross-validation. Additional validation strategies such as testing on independent external cohorts may offer more robust insights into the model’s cross-site transferability. V. C ONCLUSION 

This work proposes PopDRP, a progressive population graph learning framework for brain disease diagnosis following a disentangle–rectify–perceive pipeline. CAR extracts clinically relevant non-imaging similarities free of confounds; NGR eliminates false graph edges via minimal-sufficient represen-tation; the SPSS backbone captures multi-scale hierarchical features by integrating selective state-space modeling and U-Net graph pooling. Evaluated via 10-fold cross-validation on ABIDE and ADHD-200, PopDRP attains SOTA diagnostic accuracies, with ablation experiments verifying each mod-ule’s efficacy. It further delivers interpretable results including structured embeddings to link model outputs to clinical pop-ulation patterns, alongside biologically meaningful disease-related brain connectivity biomarkers.
