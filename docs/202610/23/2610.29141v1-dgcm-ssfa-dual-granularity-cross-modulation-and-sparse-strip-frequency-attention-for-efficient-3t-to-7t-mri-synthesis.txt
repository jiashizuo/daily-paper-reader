# DGCM-SSFA: Dual-Granularity Cross-Modulation and Sparse Strip Frequency Attention for Efficient 3T-to-7T MRI Synthesis 

Zhichun Fan, Zhiqiang Chen, Lanling Liu, Lingjun Liu, Zhonghua Xie ∗

School of Computer Science and Engineering, Huizhou University, Huizhou, China 

Abstract —Synthesizing high-fidelity 7T MRI from routinely acquired 3T scans offers a practical pathway to enhanced neuroimaging without the prohibitive cost of ultra-high-field scanners. While existing deep learning approaches have achieved promising results, state-of-the-art methods typically rely on either large-scale architectures exceeding 45 million parameters or computationally intensive ensemble strategies, limiting their practicality in resource-constrained clinical environments. In this work, we propose a compact multi-domain attention framework, termed DGCM-SSFA, that achieves competitive synthesis quality with only 11M parameters. The proposed architecture integrates two complementary modules into a U-Net Transformer backbone. First, a Dual-Granularity Cross-Modulated Feature Modulator (DGCM) is embedded at multiple hierarchical levels to enhance local edge and texture details through Sobel-guided spatial attention and depthwise frequency-aware channel attention with cross-branch gating. Second, a Sparse Strip Frequency Attention (SSFA) module at the bottleneck captures global spatial context via strip-pooled attention with near-linear complexity, while simultaneously performing FFT-based spectral modulation in the frequency domain. These two modules operate synergistically with the backbone’s native channel attention, forming a triple-granularity feature refinement pipeline. Extensive experiments on the UNC and BNU 3T-to-7T MRI datasets across T1w and T2w modalities demonstrate that DGCM-SSFA achieves strong structural fidelity, attaining an SSIM of 0.8431 on BNU T2w— approaching the best benchmark value of 0.8624—and 0.8100 on BNU T1w, while using substantially fewer parameters than Transformer-based alternatives such as ResViT (123.4M) and FS-RWKV (45.4M). These results validate that carefully designed multi-domain attention provides a practical and parameter-efficient solution for clinical MRI synthesis. 

Index Terms —MRI synthesis, 3T-to-7T translation, multi-domain attention, frequency-aware modulation, sparse strip at-tention, compact network, medical image synthesis

Despite these compelling diagnostic advantages, 7T adoption is severely limited by hardware costs exceeding several million dollars, specialized site requirements, and stringent safety protocols—factors that collectively restrict access to a handful of elite research centers worldwide [1]. Synthesizing 7T-equivalent images from routine 3T acqui-sitions has therefore attracted substantial interest [2]–[4]. The task is to learn a mapping that recovers the high-resolution anatomical details latent in clinical scans, yet this mapping must capture fine-grained edge structures at tissue boundaries, long-range anatomical dependencies across entire brain re-gions, and subtle textural variations, which remains an open challenge. Early approaches to this problem employed deep convolu-tional architectures [7]–[9] and generative adversarial formu-lations [10]–[12]. These methods exhibit a characteristic trade-off: CNNs faithfully reconstruct local intensity patterns but fail to model dependencies beyond their limited receptive field, whereas GANs can hallucinate visually convincing textures at the cost of introducing spurious anatomical features that un-dermine diagnostic trustworthiness. More recent Transformer-based designs [13]–[15] address the global context deficit through self-attention, but their quadratic complexity forces aggressive downsampling that discards precisely the high-frequency details most critical for clinical interpretation. The latest generation of state space models [16], [17] partially resolves this tension by offering linear-complexity global modeling; however, they operate predominantly in the spatial domain and provide no explicit mechanism for frequency-selective feature refinement. A deeper issue cuts across these paradigms. Anatomical structures in MRI span multiple scales and exhibit distinct signatures in both the spatial and frequency domains. Edge contours at tissue boundaries are best captured by local gradient operators, global intensity distributions across an entire slice are naturally modeled by long-range attention, and fine periodic textures are most compactly represented in the Fourier domain. Yet no existing cross-field synthesis framework integrates all three forms of attention—local edge-guided, global spatial, and frequency-domain—within a single unified architecture. The principal contributions of this work are: 1) A multi-domain attention architecture, DGCM-SSFA, that integrates dual-granularity cross-modulated feature enhancement and sparse strip frequency attention into a U-Net Transformer for 3T-to-7T MRI synthesis, operat-ing at 11M parameters. 2) The DGCM module, which unifies Sobel-structured edge priors, depthwise spectral estimation, and spatial-channel gating to amplify local anatomical detail. 3) The SSFA module, which achieves global context mod-eling with near-linear complexity through strip-pooled attention and incorporates FFT-based spectral modula-tion for frequency-aware refinement. 4) Extensive experiments on the UNC and BNU datasets across T1w and T2w modalities, demonstrating that DGCM-SSFA achieves strong structural fidelity— attaining an SSIM of 0.8431 on BNU T2w, approaching the best benchmark value of 0.8624 while using only 11M parameters, substantially fewer than Transformer-based alternatives. II. RELATED WORK 

A. Deep Learning for Cross-Field MRI Synthesis 

Mapping 3T MRI to its 7T counterpart lies at the inter-section of super-resolution and cross-modal translation. Early approaches used convolutional architectures trained with pixel-wise regression [7], [8], achieving reasonable intensity fidelity but producing over-smoothed outputs that lacked crisp anatom-ical boundaries. Adversarial training [10]–[12] substantially improved perceptual quality, but introduces a well-documented risk of hallucinating spurious anatomical features [10], [12]—a dangerous failure mode in diagnostic imaging. Transformer-based architectures [6], [13], [14] address the smoothness issue through self-attention, capturing long-range dependencies that CNNs miss, but ResViT [6] combines residual convolutional blocks with vision transformers at the cost of 123M parameters. State space formulations [2], [16], [17] offer a more efficient alternative, with FS-RWKV [2] showing that linear-complexity recurrent architectures can ap-proach Transformer-level quality. Despite these advances, the dominant design philosophy remains one of scaling, leaving a gap for compact architectures that achieve competitive quality through structural innovation. 

B. Attention Mechanisms in Image Restoration 

Channel attention, as popularized by SENet [20] and refined by CBAM [21], recalibrates feature responses by modeling interdependencies between channels through global pooling operations. Spatial attention [22] complements channel atten-tion by generating a 2D weight map that highlights informative regions; however, standard spatial attention based on max- and average-pooling statistics lacks sensitivity to structural edges, motivating the use of explicit gradient operators such as Sobel filters [23] to inject edge-awareness. A parallel line of work explores frequency-domain pro-cessing for image restoration [24]–[27]. Different degradation types manifest in distinct frequency bands: high-frequency components carry edge and texture information, while low-frequency components encode global illumination and con-trast. Fast Fourier Convolution [24] performs convolution in the complex frequency domain through a dual-branch design, whereas DCT-based learning [26] applies channel selection for efficient spectral processing. For global context aggregation, strip pooling [19] offers an attractive compromise between the limited receptive field of local operations and the quadratic cost of dense self-attention by compressing the attention token space from O(HW ) to 

O(H + W ). This technique is increasingly adopted in restora-tion tasks where global illumination and contrast consistency are critical. 

C. Multi-Domain and Multi-Granularity Feature Fusion 

A recurring theme in recent restoration literature is com-bining complementary attention mechanisms. CBAM [21] pioneered this idea by stacking channel and spatial attention, and subsequent work extended it to the frequency domain [25], [26]. Others use cross-modal gating [18], [28], where one branch modulates another. Despite these advances, existing MRI synthesis frameworks typically employ at most two atten-tion types and rarely integrate frequency-domain processing or edge-aware spatial attention within a unified design. Our work addresses this gap by orchestrating three complemen-tary attention mechanisms—local edge-guided spatial, global strip-pooled spatial, and frequency-domain spectral—coupled through cross-branch and cross-domain gating. E. Implementation Details 

DGCM-SSFA is implemented in PyTorch with the BasicSR toolbox [30], and trained with AdamW [31] (initial learning rate 3 × 10 −4, weight decay 1 × 10 −4) under a cosine annealing schedule [32]. Training runs for 30,000 iterations with a two-stage progressive patch size ( 128 × 128 for the first 10,000 iterations, then 256 × 256 ), a batch size of 8 per GPU on two NVIDIA RTX 5090 GPUs, and standard geometric augmentation (random flipping, 90-degree rotation, and cropping). IV. EXPERIMENTS 

A. Datasets and Preprocessing 

We evaluate on two publicly available paired 3T–7T MRI datasets: UNC and BNU [2]. UNC provides paired 3T (Siemens Prisma) and 7T (Siemens Terra) brain scans from 10 participants (T1w: MPRAGE/MP2RAGE; T2w: SPACE/TSE); BNU provides 20 subjects scanned on 3T Siemens Prisma and 7T Siemens Magnetom (T1w: MPRAGE/3D MPRAGE; T2w: TSE/3D SPACE). Following standard preprocessing, all volumes are resampled to isotropic 1.0 mm, skull-stripped, rigidly registered across field strengths using FSL FLIRT [33], and normalized to [0 , 1] . Each volume is sliced along the axial plane into 256 × 256 2D images. 

B. Evaluation Metrics 

We adopt three widely used quantitative metrics for im-age synthesis quality assessment: Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR), Structural Similarity Index Measure (SSIM) [34] computed with an 11 ×11 Gaussian kernel ( σ = 1 .5), and Root Mean Square Error (RMSE). PSNR and RMSE capture pixel-level reconstruction fidelity, while SSIM reflects perceptual structural consistency. Higher PSNR and SSIM values indicate better performance, whereas lower RMSE denotes superior reconstruction accuracy. 

C. Comparison with State-of-the-Art Methods 

To comprehensively assess the synthesis capability of DGCM-SSFA, we compare it against eight representative methods spanning four architectural paradigms. The CNN-based category includes DDCNN [8] and WATNet [35]; GAN-based methods comprise CycleGAN [10], pix2pix [11], and RegGAN [12]; Transformer-based approaches cover ResViT [6] and PTNet [5]; and the SSM-based baseline FS-RWKV [2] represents the latest state-space model. All methods are trained and evaluated under a unified protocol on the same data splits. 

Quantitative Results. Table I reports the numerical com-parison across four modality-site combinations. Our DGCM-SSFA achieves competitive performance with only 11M pa-rameters. On the BNU T2w dataset, it attains the second-highest SSIM of 0.8431, trailing only FS-RWKV (0.8624), demonstrating the effectiveness of the multi-granularity at-tention design in preserving structural fidelity. On the BNU T1w dataset, the SSIM of 0.8100 ranks second only to FS-RWKV (0.8388). In terms of parameter efficiency, DGCM-SSFA contains 11M parameters—significantly fewer than FS-RWKV (45.40M), ResViT (123.44M), pix2pix (57.18M), and PTNet (28.13M)—while maintaining competitive or superior structural similarity. 

Qualitative Analysis. Visual comparisons of synthesized 7T-like images are presented in Fig. 2. DGCM-SSFA recov-ers sharper tissue boundaries and finer anatomical textures compared to competing methods, particularly in cortical gray matter and subcortical regions where high-frequency details are critical. 

D. Ablation Studies 

We conduct ablation experiments on the BNU T2w dataset to validate the contribution of each core component; quan-titative results are summarized in Table II and visualized in Fig. 3. Removing all DGCM modules yields a PSNR of 25.88 dB and SSIM of 0.8206, degradations of 0.39 dB and 0.0225, confirming that the edge-aware spatial and frequency-aware channel branches jointly preserve fine anatomical boundaries. Removing SSFA gives 26.02 dB and 0.8270 (drops of 0.25 dB and 0.0161), with the degradation most pronounced in regions of elongated anatomical structures, validating the contribu-tion of strip-pooled global attention and FFT-based spectral modulation. With both modules removed, the backbone alone reaches 25.66 dB and 0.8048. The individual contributions (0.39 + 0 .25 = 0 .64 dB) closely match the joint contribution (0.61 dB), leaving only 0.03 dB of functional overlap and indicating that the two modules operate on largely orthogonal feature spaces. V. DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION DGCM-SSFA achieves competitive synthesis quality with only 11M parameters, showing that carefully designed multi-domain attention can rival larger architectures. On BNU T2w it attains the second-highest SSIM of 0.8431, approaching FS-RWKV (0.8624) while using only 24% of its parameters. The ablation study further reveals that DGCM and SSFA operate on largely orthogonal feature spaces with only 0.03 dB of functional overlap.
