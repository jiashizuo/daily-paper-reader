# fMRI-TAMCL: Text-Anchored Supervised Multimodal Contrastive Learning for fMRI-Based Brain Disorder Classification 

Juliana Mantebea Danso, Student Member, IEEE, Enoch Opanin Gyamfi, and Mylene C.Q. Farias, Senior Member, IEEE 

Abstract— Resting-state fMRI is important in the clas-sification of brain disorders, but highly multimodal and exhibits strong multisite heterogeneity. Existing meth-ods fuse images, BOLD-based functional connectivity, and phenotypic data modalities. Unlike other medical imaging datasets, rs-fMRI datasets rarely include a text modality, so they are generated from phenotypic data or BOLD activations. These text generation methods rely on fixed assumptions for subjects, sites, devices, and protocols, leading to poor generalization across datasets. We propose fMRI-TAMCL, a text-anchored multimodal contrastive learning framework that integrates fMRI images, sparse FC, and generated subject-specific text. Its Subject-Adaptive Threshold Derivation module generates BOLD activation text, while Feature–Value Serialization module generates phenotypic text. All three modalities are encoded as clustered graphs, projected onto a shared unit hypersphere space, aligned using pairwise, text-anchored supervised contrastive learning, and fused with attention. fMRI-TAMCL proves its generalization capa-bility across five datasets outperforming 29 baselines with 78.6%–86.4% accuracy in downstream classification. Index Terms— rS-fMRI, multimodal fusion, con-trastive learning, phenotypic data, AI diagnosis.

# RESTING-state functional magnetic resonance imaging (rs-fMRI) enables automated large-scale classification of brain disorders, but its use is limited by multi-site heterogeneity and mul-timodality. Public rs-fMRI datasets are collected across sites, scanner manufacturers, imaging proto-cols, and cohorts, that complicate generalizing model assumptions. Typical rs-fMRI studies include mul-tiple heterogeneous modalities: images, functional connectivity matrices (FC), and phenotypic records tabulating demographic, cognitive, behavioral, and

This unimodal approach limits the use of complementary cross-modal infor-mation. Among them, FC matrices are especially common. For instance, BrainGNN [1] learns inter-pretable region-of-interest (ROI) and community-level biomarkers from FC-derived graphs. Recent multimodal strategies tend to fuse FCs with other quantitative neuroimaging modalities (MRI, PET, DTI, EEG, and image) [2] and largely ignore text data. A critical gap in therefore created in integrating clinical text and neuroimaging data, reducing the field’s opportunity to leverage recent advances in large language models (LLMs). Contrastive learning, a common multimodal learning strategy, also offers an opportunity to fuse neuroimaging and text by aligning them in a shared embedding space, while supervised contrastive learning further improves di-agnostic class separation through label-defined pos-itive pairs [3]. These opportunities have recently motivated text-enhanced rs-fMRI diagnosis, but be-cause such datasets rarely contain subject-level text data, existing methods must derive text representa-tions from brain activations. These existing methods typically use fixed activation thresholds, mapping ROI-level BOLD activations to labels (e.g., weak, moderate, strong). They assume similar activation distributions across subjects and sites. However, in multisite datasets, a fixed threshold cannot label dif-ferent neural patterns due to inter-subject and inter-site variability in scanner and acquisition protocols. Fixed thresholds therefore introduce systematic label noise into the text modality, degrading downstream multimodal fusion. Fixed thresholds are also diffi-cult to calibrate across datasets, resulting in poor generalizability across different datasets and brain disorders. We present fMRI-TAMCL, a text-anchored multi-modal contrastive learning framework for rs-fMRI– based brain disorder diagnosis that integrates three subject-level modalities: fMRI images, sparse FC, and generated text data. The Subject-Adaptive Threshold Derivation (SATD) module models each subject’s BOLD activation distribution with aGamma–Gaussian mixture model (GGMM) and de-rives subject-level activation thresholds from the weighted component intersections, replacing fixed thresholds. The Feature-Value Serialization (FVSe-rial) module converts tabular phenotypic data also into text data. SATD+FVSerial text data are merged into structured neurobiological text data that act as semantic anchors for contrastive learning. All modalities are represented as graphs via a unified construction pipeline, modularity-based clustering, and a GNN for feature embeddings. The image, FC and text embeddings are projected onto a shared unit hypersphere latent space and optimized with three contrastive objectives: pairwise cross-modal alignment, text-anchored alignment, and supervised diagnostic class separation. An attention-based fu-sion module assigns subject-specific modality weights prior to classification. The main contributions of this study are: (1) SATD, a subject-adaptive GGMM-based threshold-ing framework that generates text data from BOLD activation, and FVSerial, which also generates text data from tabular phenotypic data. These generated text data are unified to a single semantic textual modality; (2) a graph-based multimodal feature ex-traction pipeline for images, sparse FCs, and the generated texts; (3) a text-anchored, supervised mul-timodal contrastive learning with multi-head self-attention fusion for diagnostic classification; and (4) extensive experiments on five multisite datasets eval-uating the generalization capability of the proposed model on diagnostic performance, robustness (with ablations), sensitivity, computational efficiency, and interpretability.

LLMs generally handle natural-language text more effectively than raw tabular data. Phenotypic data has rows as subjects’ record values and columns as their features. We then use a T0 encoder–decoder LLM model, a T5-based architecture trained on di-verse prompt-oriented task, to independently convert each template-instantiated sentence into a concise natural-language text with relevant clinical context. III. E XPERIMENTAL RESULTS 

We analyze five multisite rs-fMRI datasets spanning diverse brain disorders: ABIDE, ADHD-200, ADNI, REST-meta-MDD, and HCP-EP, each with rs-fMRI and phenotypic data. Preprocessing follows dataset-specific pipelines: CCS (ABIDE), Athena (ADHD-200), standard ADNI preprocessing, DPARSF [8] (REST-meta-MDD), and modified ABCD-BIDS [9] (HCP-EP). Shared steps include motion correc-tion, nuisance regression, MNI registration, tempo-ral band-pass filtering, and spatial smoothing, with dataset-specific additions such as motion scrubbing (ADNI), initial-volume removal (REST-meta-MDD), and distortion/structural correction (HCP-EP). We used an 80/20 stratified train–test split balanced by site and diagnosis across datasets. Experiments used Python 3.9.16, PyTorch 2.1, and PyTorch Geometric 2.4 on an NVIDIA RTX 4090 (CUDA 12.4). Image features came from frozen ViT-B/16 (196+1 [CLS] tokens, 768 dim) and text features from frozen BERT-base-uncased (768-dim tokens). T0-11B was fine-tuned for FVSerial. For LLSFC, α = 0 .01 , β = 0 .1, and modality graphs used K = 10 nearest neighbors. With (N (I) <N (L) < N (T )), modularity clustering used 32, 64, and 128 clusters for image, FC, and text. The 3-layer GCN had hidden sizes 64, 128 and 256, with dropout p = 0 .3, batch norm, and ReLU. Projection heads were 2-layer MLPs (256 hidden, 128 output). The contrastive temperature was τ = 0.07 . Loss weights were λpair = 1 .0, λtext = 0 .5, λsub = 0 .5,and λcon = 0 .4. Training used AdamW (learning rate 10 −4, weight decay 10 −4), batch size 32, acosine schedule, 200 epochs, and early stopping with patience 20. GGMM-based SATD used ninit = 5 EM restarts, tolerance ε = 10 −6, 200 maximum itera-tions, 2,000 grid points on [0 .001 , 2.0] , Brent–Dekker threshold refinement, and a minimum separation of 0.05 between τ1 and τ2.We compare fMRI-TAMCL with 20 unimodal and 9 multimodal baseline methods. SATD module was calibrated at subject level for fMRI-TAMCL. The yielded threshold was τ 1 = [0 .128 ± 0.047] 

and τ 2 = [0 .263 ± 0.061] . Unimodal baselines in-clude FC methods using Pearson correlation ma-trices, image-based CNN and ViT models, and a BERT text model trained on SATD+FVSerial. Mul-timodal baselines include late- and intermediate-fusion ensembles, CMT, Concat-MLP, TUMSyn, BrainPrompt+, PMIL, fTSPL [26], and RTGMFF [27]. RTGMFF is the closest baseline, as it also performs ROI-driven fMRI text generation but uses globally fixed thresholds across subjects. Table I shows that fMRI-TAMCL achieves the best diagnostic performance on all five datasets, with accuracies of 84.7%, 80.1%, 83.3%, 86.4%, and 78.6% on ABIDE, ADHD-200, ADNI, HCP-EP, and REST-meta-MDD, respectively, surpassing RTGMFF [27] by 1.6–3.0%. The largest gain on HCP-EP suggests strong generalization across heterogeneous brain dis-orders. We conducted an ablation study comparing the full model with seven variants that remove or re-place contrastive learning, text anchoring, super-vised contrastive loss, SATD, LLM-based pheno-typic serialization, modularity-based clustering, and attention-based fusion. As shown in Figure 3, the full model achieves the best accuracy on all datasets.
